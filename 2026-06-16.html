Tips for Sharing Jesus

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 12, Share Him Part 4 of 7
June 16, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Think of the people you already see every week without trying. The postal carrier. The store clerk. The neighbor walking her dog at the same time you walk yours. Peter wrote to believers surrounded by suspicious neighbors and gave them a strategy that fits those exact sidewalks: live a life so compassionate and so unintimidated that people finally ask about it, and be ready with a gentle answer when they do. Friendship first. Prayer underneath. One Bible promise at a time. Sharing Jesus is mostly being ready, being gentle, and letting your hope make someone curious.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday left us with two pictures hanging side by side: a hen with her wings spread over a city that said no, and an empire stamping incense receipts. Love compels from the inside and never forces from the outside. But spread wings still raise a practical question. On an ordinary Tuesday, with a job and a commute and a phone full of errands, what does unforced witnessing actually look like? Today things get wonderfully concrete. The Servant's promise from Sabbath returns ("the tongue of the learned... a word in season"), and Peter, the fisherman who once had no credentials, hands us the working manual he learned the hard way.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Neighbors Started Asking Questions

Picture a believer in the Roman province of Pontus around the year 64. She no longer attends the temple feasts. She does not curse the slave who breaks a jar. She visits the sick family two doors down, the one everybody else avoids, and when her husband insults her at dinner she answers him kindly. The neighbors talk. Some of the talk is ugly (Christians in this era were rumored to be atheists, since they had no statues, and worse). But some of the talk turns into a question at the kitchen door, asked half in mockery and half in wonder: what is it with you people?

That question is the entire setting of 1 Peter. The letter is addressed to scattered believers across Asia Minor, ordinary people living as a misunderstood minority under growing suspicion. Peter's strategy for them was not to argue louder or hide deeper. It was to live a visibly different life, sympathetic, humble, refusing to repay insult with insult, and then to be ready when the lifestyle provoked the question. The answer he coached them to keep ready was not a lecture. It was one sentence of honest hope, delivered gently, to a curious neighbor at the kitchen door, the market stall, the well. Twenty centuries later, the strategy has not aged a day.

📖 Into the Word

With whom are you sharing Jesus? Not in theory, but on your actual street, in your actual week? God calls every believer into this work, and He backs the call with the Sabbath promise of an instructed tongue that knows "a word in season" for the weary (Isaiah 50:4). Today's anchor, 1 Peter 3:8-15, shows what that looks like stitched into ordinary relationships, and it ends with the verse this whole day turns on: be ready to explain your hope.

Always be prepared to give a defense to everyone who asks you the reason for the hope that is in you. But respond with gentleness and respect,

Notice the order Peter builds. Seven verses of character come before one verse of speech. Sympathy, love, compassion, humility, blessing the people who insult you, seeking peace: that is the soil, and the famous "give a defense" verse (1 Peter 3:15) only grows out of it. The Greek word behind "defense" is apologia, the speech a defendant gave in court to explain himself, but Peter lifts the word out of the courtroom and hands it to ordinary believers for conversations at the kitchen door. The hope-explanation Peter describes is an answer "to everyone who asks," which quietly assumes the believer's life has been strange enough, in the loveliest way, to provoke the question. And he immediately adds how the answer must come, "with gentleness and reverence" (1 Peter 3:16). No steamrolling. No scorekeeping. A set-apart heart, a noticeable life, a gentle answer.

From this soil grow the practical habits every believer can start this week. Build a real friendship over time and let warmth do the introducing (some call it friendship evangelism). Pray for the Holy Spirit to work on the person's heart, and for the right openings. Talk naturally about what God has actually done for you, or offer to pray for them when life hits hard. Connect them with the wider warmth of your church family, a meal, a small group, a study. Share one Bible promise that meets their actual need rather than a firehose of doctrine. When the time is right, invite a next step, without rushing and without stalling. And under all of it, keep growing yourself, because a character being shaped into His likeness is the most persuasive argument you will ever carry.

🎯 Street Level
The Understudy's Knock

An understudy learns an entire starring role for a night that may never come. Months of it. Every line, every cue, every mark on the stage floor, rehearsed in empty rooms while somebody else takes the bow eight shows a week. Then one evening at 6:40 the lead's voice is simply gone, and there is a knock on the dressing room door. You're on. Here is the thing about that knock. The timing belongs to someone else. You cannot schedule it, postpone it, or talk your way out of it, and you absolutely cannot start learning the part after you hear it. The only thing you ever controlled was whether the role was already in you when it came.

Peter's "Always be prepared" works exactly like that. The question about your hope arrives on the asker's schedule, not yours. It shows up in a break room after a bad diagnosis, across a fence after a funeral, in the car line when a neighbor's marriage is quietly coming apart. There is no cramming at the kitchen door. Whatever comes out in that moment is what was already living in you, the hope you have actually rehearsed, the promise you hid in your heart months ago, the gentleness practiced across a thousand unwatched days. The knock picks the night. You pick, every quiet day before it, whether the part is learned.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Question Is Back on the Table

In 2025, the Barna Group's State of the Church project reported a number that caught nearly everyone off guard. Sixty-six percent of U.S. adults now say they have made a personal commitment to Jesus that still matters in their life, up twelve points since 2021, when the measure hit its lowest point in decades. The rise is being led by the last group anyone predicted: young men. Commitment jumped fifteen points among Gen Z men and nineteen points among Millennial men. Here is the wrinkle, though. Overall church attendance has stayed mostly flat, with young adults the main group bucking that trend, which means much of the new curiosity is stirring outside institutional doors. And when Barna asks spiritually open people what they actually want from a faith conversation, the answers keep circling the same thing: honesty about doubts, real listening, and no judgment.

Read those findings next to 1 Peter 3:15 and the letter feels like it was mailed this morning. Peter never told his people to build a more impressive institution. He told them to live lives strange enough to provoke the question and to keep a gentle answer ready, because the asking would come at kitchen doors, not temple gates. The data now describes exactly that world: millions quietly curious, waiting not for a better program but for an unhurried, unjudging answer from a familiar face. The knock is coming earlier than anyone scheduled it. The only variable Peter leaves in our hands is whether the part is learned.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Every tip on today's list is a page from Jesus' own playbook. He built friendships across every line His culture drew, eating at tables respectable rabbis avoided, until "friend of sinners" became the insult His enemies reached for. He prayed all night for the people He would meet the next day. He shared one promise at a time, living water for a thirsty woman at a well, rest for the weary, bread for the hungry. He never rushed a next step, and He never let one drift either. And when 1 Peter 3:15 tells us to "sanctify Christ as Lord" in our hearts before we ever open our mouths, it names the secret of the whole chapter, because the hope we are asked to explain is not an idea we defend. It is a Person we host. Set Him apart in your heart, and the answer at the kitchen door will take care of itself. The gentleness Peter requires is not a technique. It is what Christ in you actually sounds like.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Tonight, write your answer to one question: "Why do you have hope?" Three sentences, thirty seconds, zero churchy words, the way you would actually say it across a fence. Read it out loud once so your mouth has been there before. Then pick one tip from today's list (a friendship to invest in, a prayer for someone's heart, one Bible promise to share, an invitation to offer) and put it on your calendar for this week with a name attached.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for making this so much more ordinary than we feared. Not a stage, not a script, just our own streets and the people You have already placed on them. Make our lives the kind that provoke the question. Give us sympathy instead of sharpness, blessing instead of payback, peace that people can feel from across the room. Set Christ apart as Lord in our hearts, so that when someone finally asks about our hope, the answer is already living there. And keep us gentle, Lord. We would rather win the person than win the argument. Wake our ears for the openings You arrange this week, and give us the word in season You promised. In Jesus' name, Amen.
