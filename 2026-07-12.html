The Problem of Cliques in the Church

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 3, Unity in Christ Part 2 of 7
July 12, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Listen closely to the Corinthian church and you can hear chanting. I belong to Paul. I belong to Apollos. It sounds like team spirit, until Paul cuts in with a question no favorite teacher survives. Was he crucified for you? The divisions had already spilled into lawsuits and the communion table, and Paul treats none of it as minor. He files "strife" right next to idolatry. The fix is not picking the best leader. It is remembering that only one Person ever bled for you. Cliques feel like loyalty, but they are really the church forgetting who was crucified for it.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday we watched a whole city size people up by whose name they carried, and we saw a young church catch the same disease, wearing "Paul" or "Apollos" like a badge of rank. Today Paul quotes the slogans back to them, and you can almost hear the chant rise. I belong to Paul. I belong to Apollos. Then he interrupts the cheering with three questions that stop the room cold. Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Were you baptized in Paul's name? Every honest answer is no, and every no pulls the floor out from under the factions. This is where Paul starts his repair work, not with the surface quarrels but with the cross buried underneath them all.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
They Would Rather Sue a Brother Than Lose Face

Two men who once broke bread at the same table now stand on opposite sides of a Corinthian courtroom, in front of a pagan magistrate, airing the church's dirty laundry for the whole city to enjoy. In the Roman world this was almost ordinary. Going to court was less about justice and more about honor, a public arena where a man could humiliate a rival and climb a rung by dragging him down. The wealthy sued to flex their influence, juries could be tilted by status and quiet bribes, and the whole affair was a bloodsport of reputation. Winning was the point. Being right was optional.

That instinct did not evaporate when people were baptized. It walked straight through the church doors and sat down. A community trained from childhood to treat every relationship as a contest simply found new contests, and the slogans of chapter 1 were only the loudest symptom. Long before Paul ever mentions a courtroom or a communion table, he is dealing with people who kept score for a living and could not stop keeping score once they were saved. Their team spirit was not harmless enthusiasm. It was the first visible crack in a wall that was already coming down.

📖 Into the Word

You can hear the factions before you see them. One corner of the room belongs to Paul, another swears by Apollos, a third lines up behind Cephas, and a fourth trumps everyone by claiming to belong to Christ, as if Jesus were the most exclusive club of all. Paul has already begged them to stop splitting apart (1 Corinthians 1:10). Now he shows them how absurd their scorekeeping really is, and he does it by asking a question none of their heroes can survive. Did any of these men bleed for you? Watch him refuse to enjoy his own fan club, and watch how fast he changes the subject from himself back to the cross.

1 Corinthians 1:12-17

Were you baptized into the name of Paul? 14I thank God that I did not baptize any of you except Crispus and Gaius, 15so no one can say that you were baptized into my name. 16Yes, I also baptized the household of Stephanas; beyond that I do not remember if I baptized anyone else. 17For Christ did not send me to baptize, but to preach the gospel, not with words of wisdom, lest the cross of Christ be emptied of its power.

Paul lets them hear their own chant, then drops three questions like a hammer. Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Were you baptized in Paul's name? The answer to each is obviously no, and each no dismantles a faction. The word underneath all of it is the Greek schisma, a tear or a split, the very word you would use for ripping a piece of cloth clean in two. That is what their team spirit was quietly doing to the body of Christ. Then Paul does something almost funny. He is relieved he baptized hardly any of them, because the last thing he wants is a single soul walking around thinking they belong to him. His job was never to build a following. It was to preach a cross, and a preacher who collects fans has buried the very thing he was sent to lift up. Read the slogans again and the tragedy is plain (1 Corinthians 1:12-27).

This was not a rumor Paul went looking for. Word had reached him from Chloe's household that the church was consumed with rivalry (1 Corinthians 1:11), and the fault lines ran everywhere. Believers were hauling one another before pagan judges to win petty disputes (1 Corinthians 6:1-3), a thing Paul says should leave them ashamed (1 Corinthians 6:5). Even the Lord's Supper had curdled into a place where the divisions showed, the well-off eating their fill while the poor went hungry (1 Corinthians 11:17-22). None of it was harmless. Paul wants them to see that what they are calling loyalty keeps very dark company.

Galatians 5:20

idolatry and sorcery; hatred, discord, jealousy, and rage; rivalries, divisions, factions,

Look at the neighborhood strife lives in. When Paul lists the works of the flesh, contention and factions show up wedged between idolatry and fits of rage, filed right alongside sorcery and jealousy as things that war against the Spirit. This is the same ugliness he keeps naming in the Corinthians' quarrels, and he drops it into vice list after vice list so no one can pretend it is a minor flaw (Romans 1:29, Romans 13:13, 2 Corinthians 12:20). To Paul, a church that fights and cliques up is not merely awkward or immature in a harmless way. It is carnal, still ruled by the very flesh it was supposed to leave behind (1 Corinthians 3:3). We like to rank sins and set "division" down near the harmless end. Paul sets it next to idolatry.

🎯 Street Level
The Two Tables

Picture the first big holiday after two sisters stop speaking. Nothing gets announced. The invitations just quietly go out to two houses now instead of one, and everyone in the family understands they are supposed to pick a side. Cousins who grew up inseparable start keeping their distance. An aunt rehearses which subjects are safe to bring up. The grandmother who hosted the whole family for forty years sets a smaller table and looks at the empty chairs. And here is the strange part. Nobody feels like they are tearing the family apart. Each person is simply being loyal to the sister they love most.

That is exactly what was happening in Corinth, and exactly why Paul treats it as an emergency. Choosing a side felt like devotion. It felt like loyalty to Paul, or Apollos, or Cephas. But every time someone picked a human name to rally behind, the one family Jesus died to gather got a little more torn. Loyalty to a leader you admire is not the same thing as love for the body he pointed you to. So Paul asks the question that empties every camp. Was your favorite teacher the one nailed up for you? Only one name was ever paid in blood, and it was not the name on anybody's banner. When you catch yourself lining up against other believers, it is worth asking whether your loyalty is quietly setting a second table in a family that was only ever meant to have one.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Body That Attacks Itself

Your immune system has one job. It learns to tell you from everything that is not you, then defends you against the invaders. In an autoimmune disease that defense misfires. The body's own soldiers stop recognizing home tissue as friendly and start attacking it as though it were the enemy. In 2023 a study in The Lancet tracked the health records of 22 million people and found that roughly one in ten now lives with one of these conditions, from rheumatoid arthritis to type 1 diabetes to multiple sclerosis, and more than eighty types are known. In every case the damage does not come storming in from outside. It comes from the very cells built to protect the body turning against it.

That is the exact danger Paul saw in Corinth, and it is why he refused to treat their cliques as harmless team spirit. The church was not being killed by Rome or by persecution from the outside. It was doing it to itself, members who were meant to guard one another turning into each other's enemies, loyalty misfiring into friendly fire. A body can usually survive an outside attack. What it often cannot survive is its own defenses turned inward. When believers start treating other believers as the threat, the very love that was supposed to hold the body together becomes the thing pulling it apart. Paul drags the problem into the light because a church at war with itself is not a lively church with strong opinions. It is a body slowly consuming the one life it was given.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Go back to the one question that silenced the room. Was Paul crucified for you? He asks it because he knows the answer resets everything. No teacher, however gifted, ever hung on a cross for the Corinthians, and no leader you admire ever hung on one for you. Only Jesus did. Every faction, in the end, is a case of mistaken identity, a group of rescued people crediting the wrong rescuer. When a church splinters into camps, it is not just being petty. It is forgetting whose blood bought it. And here is the deeper wound. The cross was the place God tore heaven's best to make us one, His own Son's body broken so that enemies could be folded into a single family. To take that hard-won oneness and shred it over which speaker we prefer is to trample the very thing that saved us. Paul is not scolding them for bad manners. He is walking them back to Calvary, where the only name that matters was written in blood.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Pick a conflict you are currently invested in somewhere in the body of Christ, an online argument, a church disagreement, a grudge against another believer over how they do things. Ask yourself one blunt question about it. Did the person on the other side get crucified for you, or did Jesus? Then take one step today to lower the temperature, apologize for your part, mute the argument, or reach out to the person directly. You can hold your convictions with everything you have and still refuse to let them turn a brother or sister into an enemy.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, we confess that we love our sides. We enjoy being right, we enjoy our teams, and we have treated other believers as rivals to beat instead of family to love. Forgive us for cheapening the cross by splitting the very people it was meant to unite. Remind us today that no one else was crucified for us, only Jesus, and that His blood runs thicker than every difference we use to divide. Give us the humility to lower our weapons first. Make us people who would rather lose an argument than lose a brother or sister. In Jesus' name, Amen.
