Follow the Lamb

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 13, Into Eternity Part 5 of 7
June 24, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Ask a kid what they want to do in heaven and you get riding tigers and sliding down giraffes. Ask an adult and you get no more pain, no more goodbyes. But the Bible keeps pulling the camera back to one figure at the center: a Lamb. Not a lion, not a general, but a slain Lamb now on the throne, both the One we worship and the Shepherd who wipes every tear. The greatest thing about forever is not the scenery. It is finally thanking Him in person. The people who follow the Lamb there are the people who started following Him here.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday the city came down dressed like a bride, and we saw that the Husband at the center of it all is the Lamb who was slain to make the wedding possible. Today we walk straight up to that Lamb and look at Him. If heaven is a wedding, He is the Groom; if heaven is a city, He is its light; and it turns out He is also the Shepherd who will keep leading His people even after the journey is over. Tuesday showed us the bride being brought home. Today asks the simplest, most important question about home: who is at the center of it, and are we following Him?

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
A Nation Whose Calendar Ran on a Slain Lamb

Twice a day, every day, for roughly a thousand years, a year-old lamb was killed at the temple in Jerusalem, once at dawn and once at dusk. It was called the continual offering, and the nation's whole clock ran on it. Morning and evening were literally measured by the smoke of a lamb. On top of that came the Day of Atonement, the various guilt offerings, and above all Passover, when each household killed its own lamb. By the first century, historians estimate that hundreds of thousands of Passover lambs were slaughtered in Jerusalem in a single spring week. The streets ran with it. Everyone, from the priest to the smallest child, knew exactly what a lamb was for: a lamb died so that someone else could live.

So when a wild-looking prophet at the Jordan River pointed at his own cousin walking by and said, "Look, the Lamb of God," it was not a gentle compliment. It was a thunderclap. In a culture soaked in the blood of lambs, calling a man the Lamb meant only one thing: this is the One who is going to be sacrificed. Hold that against the strangest fact in the book of Revelation. When John is finally shown the throne room at the center of the universe, the One being worshiped is not pictured as a lion or a king on a war-horse. He is pictured as a Lamb, standing as though it had been slain. Heaven's highest seat belongs to the gentlest, most sacrificed creature in the ancient world.

📖 Into the Word

There is so much to look forward to: a world with no more death, no more goodbyes, no more hospital waiting rooms. But today's reading keeps gently redirecting our wonder away from the gifts and toward the Giver. Every blessing of eternity, it turns out, is really a description of one Person, the Lamb, who is at once the One we will worship, the One we follow, and the Shepherd who never stops leading us home.

Isaiah 25:8
☩ Open in Bible App
He will swallow up death forever. The Lord GOD will wipe away the tears from every face and remove the disgrace of His people from the whole earth. For the LORD has spoken.

Start with the blessing everyone longs for. Isaiah promises a day when God will "swallow up death once and for all," the same promise John echoes in the new earth where there is no more death or grief or crying or pain (Revelation 21:4). But notice how personal the picture is. God does not just abolish death by decree from a distance. He kneels down and "will wipe away the tears from every face," the way a parent wipes a crying child's cheeks with their own thumb. The end of suffering is not described as a policy change. It is described as the tenderest gesture one person can make toward another. Even the biggest blessing of eternity, the death of death itself, comes to us through the hands of a Person who gets close enough to touch our faces.

Revelation 5:12
☩ Open in Bible App
In a loud voice they were saying: “Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and riches and wisdom and strength and honor and glory and blessing!”

This is the song of heaven, and it is worth noticing what it praises Him for. Not "worthy is the Lamb who is strong," but "Worthy is the Lamb who was slain." The wounds are not hidden in glory; they are the reason for the worship. The word John uses for the Lamb all through Revelation is the Greek arnion, a tender little word, almost "little lamb," and he uses it of the One seated on the throne of the universe. That is the deliberate shock of the whole book: ultimate power wearing the form of ultimate gentleness, the slain made supreme. The greatest blessing of heaven will be standing in that throng and finally getting to thank Him out loud, in person, for what He did on a fallen earth. Every other joy of eternity is a wave; this is the ocean.

Revelation 14:4
☩ Open in Bible App
These are the ones who have not been defiled with women, for they are virgins. They follow the Lamb wherever He goes. They have been redeemed from among men as firstfruits to God and to the Lamb.

The first line uses the Bible's old picture of faithfulness, where loving God with an undivided heart is "purity" and chasing other gods is "adultery"; it is the same bride-language from yesterday, describing people whose devotion has not been split. But the heart of the verse is the middle sentence: "They follow the Lamb wherever He goes." It was John the Baptist who first called Jesus "the Lamb of God" (John 1:36), and the moment two of his disciples heard it, they simply got up and followed Jesus (John 1:37). That is the whole Christian life in one motion. And here is the sober, hopeful catch: following the Lamb "wherever He goes" in heaven is just the continuation of following Him here. Heaven is not where we suddenly start wanting to be near Him. It is where the following we began on earth finally has no obstacles left.

Revelation 7:17
☩ Open in Bible App
For the Lamb in the center of the throne will be their shepherd. ‘He will lead them to springs of living water,’ and ‘God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.’”

Here is the image that holds the whole day together, and it is gloriously mixed on purpose: "the Lamb... will shepherd them." The Lamb is the Shepherd. The One who was led to slaughter is the One who now leads the flock. And catch that He keeps shepherding in heaven; the leading does not stop when the journey is over. We will still be following Him to ever-fresh springs, still being guided, forever moving deeper into His presence rather than settling into a static reward. The defining mark of His people in that world is simple: His name on their foreheads (Revelation 22:4), meaning we belong to Him and our minds are finally, fully fixed on Him. The same gentle voice we have been straining to hear through the hard times will be the voice we follow to the water, and the hand that wipes the last tear will be a scarred one.

🎯 Street Level
Eyes on the Lamp

Anyone who has gone deep into a cave with a guide knows the rule that keeps you alive down there. You stay close, and you keep your eyes on their lamp. When the passage narrows and the light from the entrance is long gone and the dark is the most total dark you have ever felt, you do exactly what the guide does, step where they step, and you do not wander off to look at something on your own. And here is what the good guides know. The trust that carries you through the tight, frightening stretch is not something you can suddenly manufacture once you are in it. You build it in the wide, easy passages near the front, where following them costs you almost nothing. By the time the cave gets serious, following is already what your feet do.

That is the quiet, hopeful catch in today's reading. The redeemed are described as the ones who "follow the Lamb wherever He goes," and the surprise is that this following is not something that switches on when we get to heaven. It is the same following we are learning right now, on the ordinary days, when we keep our eyes on Him through stretches that feel dark and tight. We will not arrive at the gates and abruptly decide to trust Him. We will arrive already trusting Him, because we have been keeping our eyes on the lamp the whole way in. And the best news is who is holding it. The Lamb turns out to be the Shepherd, and the One we have been following through the dark has been leading us, the whole time, toward the water and the light.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
What the Astronauts Saw

Many astronauts who have looked back at Earth from space come home trying to describe the same thing, and often struggle to find the words. The writer Frank White gave the experience a name: the overview effect. Seeing the whole planet hanging in the black, a fragile blue marble with no visible borders, tends to flood a person with awe and a strange, tearful sense of connection to something far bigger than themselves. In one study, researchers led by David Yaden described it as awe with self-transcendent qualities, and some astronauts describe lasting changes afterward, finding they care less about the small things than they once did, pulled toward something more. They went up as pilots and engineers and came down as something closer to mystics.

That reaction is a clue about how we are built. We were made to look at something far greater than ourselves and be undone by it, made with a homesickness for a bigger picture that the daily grind can never quite satisfy. Today's reading puts a face on that "something more." At the center of everything, John keeps seeing not a planet but a Person, the Lamb, and the redeemed are simply the ones who cannot stop following Him. The awe those astronauts felt looking down at the world is a faint rehearsal for the awe of one day looking up into His face. We were built for the overview, and the view turns out to be a Person.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Every other day this week has pointed forward to the future; today plants the future squarely in the past, at a cross. The reason the Lamb is worthy to receive all power and worship is not that He is impressive. It is that He was slain. For a thousand years, lambs died at that temple and could never actually take away sin; they were rehearsals, pointing forward to a Lamb who could. When John the Baptist said "the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world," he was announcing that the rehearsals were over and the real sacrifice had arrived. So the worship of heaven is not generic praise of a powerful being. It is the eternal gratitude of rescued people to the One who took their place. And the Shepherd leading us to the water in glory still carries the marks of the day He became the Lamb. We do not follow Him into eternity because He is strong. We follow Him because He was slain, and He loved us enough to do it.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Following the Lamb in heaven starts with following Him today. Pick one place in your life right now where you sense Him leading and you have been dragging your feet: a conversation you have been avoiding, a habit He keeps nudging, a person He keeps putting on your heart. Take one small step in that direction today, on purpose, as practice. Then spend two minutes simply thanking Him, out loud if you can, for being the Lamb who was slain for you. Rehearse now the gratitude you will one day get to give Him in person.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Worthy is the Lamb who was slain. Jesus, we are amazed that the One on the throne of the universe still bears the wounds He took for us, and that the same gentle Lamb is the Shepherd who leads us to water and dries our tears. Teach us to follow You here, in the small and ordinary places, so that following You there will feel like coming home and not like meeting a stranger. Thank You that the greatest thing waiting for us is not a place but Your face, and the chance to finally thank You in person. Until then, lead us, and we will follow. In Jesus' name, Amen.
