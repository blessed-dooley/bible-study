Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 11, Setbacks Part 7 of 7
June 12, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 Week in Review

Six days, six setbacks, and one Face that kept showing up in every storm we walked through.

  • ☩Sabbath: Setbacks. A girl lifts her face into a downpour because she has decided the storm is God taking her picture, and we asked what we do with our own faces when life turns on us. Setbacks are not heaven's report card.
  • ☩Sunday: Life's Storms. The boat was swamping and Jesus was asleep in the stern, and the disciples' real fear was not the wind but the suspicion that He had stopped caring. A setback shows us what we secretly believe about God.
  • ☩Monday: Be Made Well. A woman who had bled for twelve years reached for the hem, and His wholeness ran backward into her faster than her uncleanness could reach Him. A worn-out faith is still enough.
  • ☩Tuesday: Job. Stripped of everything and unable to find God in any direction, Job still said "I know that my Redeemer lives." Faith is holding on to who God is when you cannot feel Him at all.
  • ☩Wednesday: The Road to Emmaus. Two disciples spoke their hope in the past tense, and Jesus walked them back through Scripture to show that the cross they thought ended everything was the plan all along.
  • ☩Thursday: See Jesus. Paul weighed real suffering against coming glory and called it "not worth comparing," because God works every piece together for good, and one look at the face of Christ outweighs the whole climb.
🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Verdict the Whole Week Refused

String the settings of this week together and one assumption keeps trying to speak. On the storm-prone lake, in Job's friends' speeches, on the Emmaus road, and in the empire that crucified a would-be Messiah, the ancient reflex was the same. Sudden ruin meant heaven was displeased and someone was being paid back. Job's friends said it to his face. The disciples in the boat heard it in Jesus' silence. The two on the road read a cross as proof the whole thing had failed. It was the common sense of the age, that suffering is a verdict.

What is striking is that the Bible, written inside that world, keeps refusing its logic. It shows God asleep in the boat instead of standing on the shore with a ledger. It lets Job be declared innocent over his friends. It puts the risen Christ on the road with the very people whose hope had collapsed. The setback is never treated as the final word on whether God loves us, because the God of these pages does not change, and His love does not rise and fall with the weather.

💡 What This Week Clarifies

Lay the six days beside each other and they start talking to one another. An obedient boat takes on water. An instant healing sits next to years of heaven's silence. Two grieving friends hold every fact and no hope. A week this honest about setbacks ties a few knots on purpose. Three of them are worth untying before the week lets go.

The Swamped Boat Was Following Orders

Crossing the lake that night was not the disciples' idea. Jesus gave the order to head for the other side, and the water started coming in while they were carrying it out. If setbacks were verdicts on disobedience, the one boat on the lake doing exactly what He said should have had the driest crossing. Instead it was the one going under.

Mark 4:35-41
☩ Open in Bible App
35When that evening came, He said to His disciples, “Let us cross to the other side.” 36After they had dismissed the crowd, they took Jesus with them, since He was already in the boat. And there were other boats with Him. 37Soon a violent windstorm came up, and the waves were breaking over the boat, so that it was being swamped. 38But Jesus was in the stern, sleeping on the cushion. So they woke Him and said, “Teacher, don’t You care that we are perishing?” 39Then Jesus got up and rebuked the wind and the sea. “Silence!” He commanded. “Be still!” And the wind died down, and it was perfectly calm. 40“Why are you so afraid?” He asked. “Do you still have no faith?” 41Overwhelmed with fear, they asked one another, “Who is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?”

Mark answers with seating assignments. Jesus was not on the shore watching the crew pay for something. He was in the stern of the same swamping boat, so unthreatened by weather on His own route that He slept in it. When He stood up He did not apologize for the itinerary. He muzzled the sea like an animal that had slipped its leash, then went after the deeper leak, the story the men were telling themselves about Him, that His sleep meant He had stopped caring. Here is the week's first clarification. A storm can sit inside the will of God. The obedient are not exempt from weather. They are accompanied in it. His question about their fear was never about wind. It was about Him.

She Found the Hem. Job Found Nothing

Monday and Tuesday sit shoulder to shoulder on purpose. A woman brushes the edge of a robe and twelve years of bleeding stop inside a second, with a family word spoken over her in the street. The next day Job reaches east, west, north, and south, and his hands come back empty. No voice, no verdict, no healing anywhere in the chapter. The week called both of these faith. Side by side they look more like a promise and its exception.

Job 23:8-12
☩ Open in Bible App
8If I go east, He is not there, and if I go west, I cannot find Him. 9When He is at work in the north, I cannot behold Him; when He turns to the south, I cannot see Him. 10Yet He knows the way I have taken; when He has tested me, I will come forth as gold. 11My feet have followed in His tracks; I have kept His way without turning aside. 12I have not departed from the command of His lips; I have treasured the words of His mouth more than my daily bread.

The week never located faith in the outcome. Monday praised a reach, not a result the woman could see in advance, an ember of trust half buried under twelve years of ash. Tuesday moved the same grip into the dark. Job searches every direction and finds nothing, and then the passage quietly reverses who is looking for whom. "He knows the way I have taken." Job cannot see God, but Job has never once been out of God's sight. So he keeps walking a road he learned in the light, treasuring the words more than his bread, and the week honors that walking as the same faith that reached for the hem. Some setbacks end in a second on a crowded street. Some outlast every feeling that started them. The reach and the walk are one grip. Faith holds on before the answer comes.

They Had Every Fact and Walked the Wrong Way

Wednesday's travelers were not short on information. A powerful prophet, an execution, a third morning, an empty tomb, angels reported by women they trusted. They recited every piece on the road and kept walking away from Jerusalem anyway, with hope in the past tense. The facts were complete and the story still felt finished, because the fact at the center, the cross, looked like the piece that ended it.

Romans 8:28
☩ Open in Bible App
And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose.

Thursday set the week's boldest sentence underneath that collapse. Notice what the verse does not say. It never claims all things are good, and the week never asked anyone to call them good. Raiders, bleeding, ten graves, a cross, none of it is good in itself. The claim is about what God does with such things. On the road the risen Jesus walked two broken friends back through Moses and the prophets until the piece that looked most like defeat turned out to be the center of the design. The worst thing ever done on this earth was already working for the good of the world by the third morning. That is the proof buried under the promise. Nothing on your list is too bitter for the loaf.

That reframing is the week's parting gift. The storm ends in a flat calm. Twelve years end in the word "daughter." The silence ends in gold, and the buried hope ends at a supper table in Emmaus. Not one of them gets the last word. The girl in the rain had it right from the start. When life turns on us, the face still goes up.

🌍 Still Speaking
Which Way the Face Turns

Nobody gets a life without setbacks. The lake turns on everybody eventually, and the only real question is the one the girl in the rain answered without thinking. Which way does the face turn when the storm hits? Down, into the pelting and the resentment and the quiet verdict that God has stopped caring? Or up, toward the One who has been in the boat the whole time? This week did not pretend the storms are small. A swamping boat, twelve years of bleeding, ten graves in a day, a buried hope, a brutal climb. It simply insisted that none of them is the final word on whether God is good.

There is an old picture of a believer being led up a steep and frightening road, the kind where looking down would finish you, given a single instruction the whole way. Keep your eyes lifted. At the top the traveler sees the face of Jesus, and in that instant the exhaustion of the entire climb evaporates, not because it was not real but because the joy is so much greater. That is not denial. It is perspective, the same perspective Paul had when he weighed his scars against the coming glory and called it no contest. The setbacks are real today. They are also temporary. And the Face at the top is turned toward you with joy, the same way a girl once decided the lightning was God taking her picture.

✝️ Christ at the Center of the Week

Trace one Person through the whole week and the lesson stops being about coping and starts being about Him. Sunday, He is the God who set the boundaries of the sea, asleep in the back of a fishing boat, so unafraid of the storm He can rest in it. Monday, He is the One whose wholeness runs backward down the line, taking our uncleanness into Himself and sending healing out. Tuesday, He is the living Redeemer Job longed to see, the Kinsman who would stand on the dust at the end. Wednesday, He is the risen Stranger on the road, reframing the worst setback in history as the hinge the whole story turns on. Thursday, He is the wounded Face at the top of the climb, the One who did not spare Himself. Every day this week, the answer to the setback was not a formula. It was Jesus, who walked all the way down into the deepest setback there is, a sealed tomb, and walked back out, so that He could meet us in ours with scars that prove He understands.

✅ Weekend Practice
Two things this weekend. First, find the Romans 5:3-5 card you wrote on Sabbath and read it again, out loud, naming where you have actually seen pressure doing its slow work in you this week. Second, pick one weary or hurting person, and send them a short message today carrying the news that God's power shows up strongest in the weak place. Be the friend on the Emmaus road for somebody else.
🙏 Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You for a week that refused to lie to us about pain. The storms are real, the wounds are real, the graves are real, and You never asked us to pretend otherwise. Thank You that through all of it You do not change, that You are asleep in our boat and alive on our road and waiting at the top of our climb. Teach us to turn our faces up. Pour Your love so deeply into us that our hope outlasts the weather, and make us people who walk beside the heartbroken with good news in our mouths. We will see Your face, and one look will be worth the whole climb. In Jesus' name, Amen.
