The Risen Christ, Our Only Hope!

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 8, The Power of Christ's Resurrection Part 3 of 7
August 17, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

A few Corinthian believers had found a slogan they thought sounded wise. There is no resurrection of the dead. Paul does not shout at them. He borrows the idea and walks it to the end of its road, past an unraised Christ, empty preaching, faith holding air, lying apostles, unforgiven sin, until it stops at the graves of everyone they had ever loved. Gone, on their own theory. Then he turns around and points back at the open tomb. If Christ is not risen, we have nothing. Because He is risen, we have everything that matters.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday ended in a courtroom, five hundred living witnesses deep, with Paul all but daring Corinth to go knock on doors and ask. Maybe you even sent your own small testimony out into the world, one text message about what you have seen God do. Today the tone changes. Paul stops presenting evidence and turns to face the people shrugging at it, the ones repeating that there is no resurrection of the dead. He will not mock them. He will do something far more unsettling. He will take their idea seriously, all the way down.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Soul Was Supposed to Escape

Many educated Greeks inherited philosophical traditions that treated the soul as the truest self and the body as a temporary confinement. Death could then look like release. Influential schools in the empire taught people to hope for escape from the body, not its return. So when Paul preached bodily resurrection to the intellectuals of Athens, they did not get angry. They laughed at him (Acts 17:32). Who wants the corpse back? To many Greek hearers, resurrection was not too good to be true. It was too crude to be desirable, salvation running in reverse.

Now put that schooling inside the Corinthian church. These believers loved Jesus, but they had been raised on soul-escape, and old maps die hard. Resurrection of the dead sounded embarrassing at dinner parties, so some quietly reclassified it. A metaphor, perhaps. A spiritual truth about the inner life. Nothing so vulgar as graves opening. Paul refuses the trade, because Christianity's hope was never a soul slipping out of creation. It is God raising His creation, body included, because He made it, He called it good, and He does not surrender it. That collision, Greek escape versus biblical rescue, is the fight underneath every verse today.

📖 Into the Word

Somewhere in the room when this letter was first read aloud sat the people who had actually said it. There is no resurrection of the dead. They were not trying to attack Jesus. They thought they were being sophisticated. Paul hears it as a crack running through a load-bearing wall, and rather than raise his voice, he does something colder and braver. He accepts their premise for the length of eight verses and walks it to the end of its road (1 Corinthians 15:9-19). Come along, he says, into the world you are proposing. It is a shorter, darker walk than they think.

1 Corinthians 15:12
☩ Open in Bible App
But if it is preached that Christ has been raised from the dead, how can some of you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?

You can hear Paul's bewilderment coming through the ink. He has just finished the witness list, Peter and the Twelve and the five hundred (1 Corinthians 15:5-8), and now he asks how a church built on that testimony can shrug at resurrection itself. Notice the trap they had wandered into. They wanted to keep Christ's rising as the one glorious exception while denying that resurrection ever happens. Paul closes that door with two quiet hinges. If there is no resurrection of the dead, then Christ has not been raised (1 Corinthians 15:13), and if the dead are not raised, not even Christ (1 Corinthians 15:16). His rising was not a special effect. It was the first case of the very thing they were denying. There is no keeping Jesus alive inside a worldview where the dead stay dead.

So Paul itemizes the cost of their idea, seven consequences in seven relentless lines (1 Corinthians 15:12-19). No risen Christ. Empty preaching. Empty faith. Apostles unmasked as men who testified falsely about God Himself (1 Corinthians 15:15). Futile faith. Unforgiven sin. The believing dead, gone. Then he slows down on the two lines that cut deepest.

1 Corinthians 15:14
☩ Open in Bible App
And if Christ has not been raised, our preaching is worthless, and so is your faith.

The word Paul reaches for paints a container with nothing inside. Take away the resurrection and the sermon becomes a speech about nothing, and faith becomes a hand closed around air. Notice that he does not soften this for the sake of morale, and he includes himself in the wreckage. Our proclamation, he writes, my life's work along with your believing. Readers have long debated the exact shade of that emptiness, whether it means untrue, or without effect, or without purpose, and the honest answer is that a dead Christ delivers all three at once. A message can be sincere, ancient, beautiful, and comforting, and still be an elegantly wrapped empty box. Only one thing rescues preaching and believing from that verdict. The thing preached has to have happened.

1 Corinthians 15:17
☩ Open in Bible App
And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins.

Here Paul sharpens the point with the Greek word mataios, which means fruitless, the label for effort that comes home empty-handed. Without the resurrection, faith is a field that never yields a harvest, because the one thing it was planted for, forgiveness, never actually arrived. Feel the weight of still in your sins. It would mean the cross was love poured out with nothing to show for it, and every confession you ever prayed changed nothing. Then comes the line that would have stopped every reader with a grave to visit. Those who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished (1 Corinthians 15:18). The grandmother who prayed for you. The friend who fell asleep trusting Jesus. Gone, on the no-resurrection theory, and our hope would deserve pity, not respect. Paul shows Corinth the whole bill on purpose. Without Easter morning, Good Friday is just a Friday.

🎯 Street Level
The Person Behind the Profile

By now everyone knows a version of the story, from a documentary or from someone they love. A person meets someone online, and for a year the relationship is the best thing in their life. Good-morning texts. Long calls after midnight. Inside jokes, plans, a future taking shape. Then a detail snags, a friend starts digging, and the floor gives way. There is no such person. The photos belonged to a stranger. And here is the cruelest part. Nothing they felt was fake. The love was real, the hope was real, the hours were real, and every bit of it lands on nothing, because the one fact underneath it all, that somebody was actually there, was false. Real devotion multiplied by a missing person equals zero.

Paul makes Corinth run that same arithmetic on the faith itself. If Christ has not been raised, sincerity rescues nothing. The sermons were really preached, the tears really fell, the believing was honest, and none of it lands anywhere, because there is no one on the other end. He would rather the church feel that cold once than build on it forever. But the reason he can be this ruthless is that he checked. This is the one story where the Person stepped out from behind the profile, in daylight, and was seen. Peter saw Him. James saw Him. Five hundred people at once saw Him, and Paul met Him on an open road with witnesses standing by. So the dark math runs in reverse, and everything built on Him holds. Sincerity was never the foundation. He is.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Truck Was Only Rolling Downhill

A few years ago an electric-truck startup named Nikola released a promotional video of its gleaming hydrogen semi cruising down a desert highway. Investors saw the future and bought in. By the summer of 2020 the market valued the company around thirty billion dollars, briefly more than Ford, before it had delivered a single production truck. Then a short-selling research firm, Hindenburg Research, published what insiders already knew. The truck in the video had no working powertrain. A crew had towed it to the top of a long grade and let it roll, with the camera angled so the road looked level. Founder Trevor Milton was convicted of securities fraud in 2022 and sentenced to four years. The company limped on, but on February 19, 2025, Nikola filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy with about 47 million dollars left, a fraction of a fraction of what the market once believed. The motion in the video was real footage of a real truck. It was just never driving.

Paul runs the same inspection on his own enterprise, centuries ahead of schedule. If Christ has not been raised, the whole faith is a rolling truck. The preaching was real preaching, the believing was real believing, the sacrifices were real sacrifices, and all of it would be coasting on a slope, impressive at a distance, powerless at the core, stopping wherever gravity decides. That is what his word for futile means, effort that comes home empty-handed, and Paul itemizes the cruelest line himself, still in your sins, your dead simply gone. Notice that he publishes the damaging report on his own gospel before any skeptic could, then stakes the verdict on evidence. The engine has been inspected. Witnesses saw the risen Christ, touched Him, ate with Him, and kept on testifying in public through opposition and at great personal cost. And the gospel has climbed hills for two thousand years, against the grade, through persecution, past every confident prediction of its end. Rolling things eventually stop. This has not.

✝️ The Christ Connection

There is a hidden autobiography inside today's argument. Paul opened this passage calling himself the least of the apostles, a former persecutor remade only by grace (1 Corinthians 15:10). Now watch what his own logic does to his own story. If Christ is still dead, then no one met Paul on the Damascus road. No light, no voice, no commission, no grace. Just a brilliant man who wrecked his life for a hallucination. The apostle writing this chapter is himself walking evidence that Jesus is alive, a persecutor turned proclaimer by a Person he insists he saw. And that is why the resurrection is not one hopeful doctrine among several. A living Christ guarantees forgiveness now, resurrection later, and reunion after that, because the One making the promises has already walked out of His own grave. He is not our best option among many. He is the only door that was ever open, and it is open.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Take a notebook or your phone and finish this sentence honestly. If Christ were still in His grave, the first thing I would lose is ______. Sit with what you wrote for one full minute. Then turn it into a spoken prayer of thanks for that exact thing, because He is not in His grave, and it is not lost. Carry that one sentence of thanks with you through the rest of today.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, today we walked to the edge Paul showed us and looked over it, into a world with a sealed tomb, empty sermons, unforgiven sin, and graves that keep everything they take. Thank You that we do not live in that world. Thank You that the long walk ends at a doorway with the stone rolled aside. Forgive us for the days we live like citizens of the hopeless world anyway, anxious and grasping, as if everything depended on what our hands can hold. Teach us the difference between a wish and a hope with a foundation under it. Comfort those of us missing someone who fell asleep trusting You, and keep their names safe until the morning You wake them. We hand You our doubts, our grief, and our small flickering courage. Be our only hope, and be enough. In Jesus' name, Amen.

"When you imagine the moment a believer dies, what picture do you actually carry (an immediate arrival in heaven, a dreamless sleep until the trumpet, something fuzzier you have never examined)? And if the dead in Christ are truly resting until resurrection morning, what does that change about how you grieve, and what would you say differently at the next graveside you stand beside?"
