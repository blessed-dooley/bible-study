Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 1, Reality Check Part 7 of 7
April 3, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌Week in Review

This week asked one uncomfortable question and then spent six days answering it: What is your relationship with God actually like right now?

  • ☩Sabbath: Reality Check. The week opened with the memory text from John 15:4 and established the stakes: before we can grow, we have to honestly assess where we stand. Are we connected to the Vine, or just near it?
  • ☩Sunday: Our Condition. Jesus' letter to Laodicea named the danger of lukewarm faith: the church felt rich and self-sufficient but could not see that it was poor, blind, and naked. The hardest part of healing was admitting the diagnosis.
  • ☩Monday: Rebuke, Repent, and Reward. Jesus revealed that His hard diagnosis came from love, then stood at the door and offered repentance, restored fellowship, and a place at His table. He knocks rather than forcing the handle.
  • ☩Tuesday: Everlasting Love. God's pursuing love was the focus, from Eden through Jeremiah to Revelation. He has always been the One who initiates, and His love predates our response.
  • ☩Wednesday: Abide. Jesus' Vine and Branches discourse in John 15 revealed the single word that defines our side of the relationship: remain. Ten repetitions. No ambiguity.
  • ☩Thursday: Linked to the Vine. The Holy Spirit is the sap that flows from the Vine into the branches. We cannot produce fruit through effort; we receive it through the Spirit's indwelling presence.
🗺️Historical & Cultural Context
A Week Between Two Gardens

This week's journey has traced a line between two gardens. It began in Eden, where God walked with humanity in the cool of the day and called out "Where are you?" when the relationship was broken. It ended on the road to Gethsemane, where Jesus spoke of vines and branches on the night before He would be crushed like grapes in a winepress. Between those two gardens lies the entire story of Scripture: God pursuing, humanity hiding, and God pursuing still.

The first-century world Jesus spoke into was full of people who had religion without relationship. The Pharisees had meticulous rule-keeping but no joy. The Laodicean church had wealth and respectability but Jesus was standing outside knocking. What this week revealed is that the ancient problem and the modern problem are the same: proximity to God is not the same as connection to God. The branch can be physically attached to the vine and still be hydraulically isolated, still producing no fruit, still slowly drying out from the inside. The reality check Jesus calls for is not about religious activity. It is about the sap.

💡 What This Week Clarifies

Reality Check is a bruising name for a week, and this one earned it. Lukewarm, wretched, and blind were the opening words, dinner and a throne were the closing offers, and a Vine ran through the middle of everything. Three tensions deserve honest answers before Sabbath comes.

The Hard Words Are the Knock

The letter to Laodicea swings between two extremes with almost no warning. Lukewarm faith turns the stomach of the Amen, and a few verses later the same voice proposes dinner. Either this church makes Him sick or He wants to sit at their table, and the letter insists on both.

Revelation 3:19
☩ Open in Bible App
Those I love I rebuke and discipline. Therefore be earnest and repent.

This is the hinge the whole letter turns on, and the week kept coming back to it. The rebuke is not the opposite of the dinner invitation. It is the reason for it. The Greek behind discipline is a parenting word, the correction given to a child too loved to be left sleepwalking, and the week's plainest picture was the check engine light. The light is not the enemy of the car. It is the mercy that comes on before the engine seizes. A coach who never corrects is not kind, only indifferent, and staying silent about a fixable problem is abandonment with good manners. So the severity and the affection are one motion. He names wretched, pitiful, poor, blind, and naked precisely because He intends to supply gold, garments, and salve from His own hand. Nobody rebukes a church they have written off. The hard words are love refusing to whisper.

There Is No Third Kind of Branch

Laodicea had built a life in the middle. Not hostile to Jesus, not on fire, just comfortable, funded, and busy, a faith running on reserve power. Most of us know that neighborhood. The week's most unsettling move was to walk through it and take the middle ground off the map.

John 15:6-8
☩ Open in Bible App
6If anyone does not remain in Me, he is like a branch that is thrown away and withers. Such branches are gathered up, thrown into the fire, and burned. 7If you remain in Me and My words remain in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you. 8This is to My Father’s glory, that you bear much fruit, proving yourselves to be My disciples.

Jesus counts the outcomes, and there are two. A branch remains and bears much fruit, or it lets go and withers, and no vineyard on earth has ever grown a third kind. That is not a threat. It is botany. A branch is either drawing sap this moment or drying this moment, whatever its leaves look like from the road. Laodicea's own plumbing preached the same sermon, since hot water heals and cold water refreshes but the tepid water that reached the city only nauseated. And the middle carries one more danger the week refused to soften. It numbs. A church can coast so smoothly it never notices the engine dying, which is why "you do not realize" was the sharpest phrase in the whole diagnosis. The honest mirror is not cruelty. Losing the imaginary middle is how the real choice comes back into view.

The Branch Does Not Push the Sap

One demand could crush a reader by Friday. Burn hot, bear fruit, remain, repent, overcome. Yet the same week said a branch cannot force sap through its own veins, and that apart from the Vine we can do nothing. The assignment sounds like willpower, and willpower is exactly the offline, reserve-battery living the week diagnosed.

Luke 11:13
☩ Open in Bible App
So if you who are evil know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!”

Jesus ends the tension with a gift. The temperature, the fruit, the power to overcome, none of it is generated by the branch, because the Father gives the Holy Spirit, the living sap, to anyone who asks. Thursday's vineyard science made the direction of flow unmistakable. A dormant winter bud sits sealed off from the vine, looking dead, unable to reconnect itself, and reconnection is not something the bud performs. The vine pushes life upward from the roots and rebuilds the channel from the inside. Our verbs all week were the small, receivable kind. Ask. Open. Remain. His verbs carry the power. Give, flow, raise the dead. Even the door in Laodicea obeys the same rule, because the one thing this pursuing love will not do is turn the handle for us. He knocks. We open. The sap does the rest.

Which brings the week back to its envelope. Sabbath asked for an honest guess at what Jesus would write in a letter to us, sealed and set aside. Six days later that guess can be measured against the real thing, a letter that diagnoses without flinching, loves without conditions, knocks without forcing, and offers His own gold, His own garments, His own Spirit. The reality check turned out to be a mercy, not an ambush. Tomorrow is Sabbath, and the practice below asks one more honest question with the pen still in hand. However the temperature reads tonight, the Vine is already pushing sap toward every branch that stays put and asks. Open the envelope. Then open the door.

🌍Still Speaking
The Dry Branch Does Not Need a New Strategy

Before we were born, God loved us. He had a plan to know us and for us to know Him. He seeks us out, like a good Shepherd, and invites us to abide in Him every day. We merely need to choose to respond to Him, and then to exchange our wretchedness and our Laodicean condition for His good gifts. That exchange does not happen through a new program, a better Bible reading plan, or a more disciplined prayer schedule. It happens the way a dry grapevine branch comes back to life in spring: the vine pushes sap upward through the roots, and the branch simply receives it.

Our culture is obsessed with self-improvement, optimization, and personal productivity. We bring that same mindset into our spiritual lives and wonder why it feels like drudgery. But the vine-and-branch relationship is not a productivity hack. It is a love story. The branch clings to the vine. The vine provides the life. Fiber by fiber, vein by vein, the branch holds fast to the life-giving vine until the life of the vine becomes one with the branch, and the branch produces fruit like that of the vine. That is not effort; that is intimacy. And it is available to anyone willing to stop performing and start receiving.

✝️Christ at the Center of the Week

Every day this week pointed to the same Person. On Sabbath, He was the standard against which we measured our reality. On Sunday, He was the Amen whose diagnosis exposed Laodicea's true condition. On Monday, He was the loving Reprover who knocked at the door and offered a place at His table. On Tuesday, He was the pursuing Lover who called out in the garden and drew us with everlasting love. On Wednesday, He was the true Vine who said "remain in me" ten times because He knew we needed to hear it that many. On Thursday, He was the One who sent the Spirit, the living sap, so that every dry branch could come back to life.

Before we can start growing in a relationship with God, we first have to pause and consider what our current relationship with Him is actually like. If it is Laodicean, if our branches are not flourishing, if we feel spiritually dry and disconnected, Jesus has the perfect solution for our condition. It is not a program. It is not a formula. It is a Person. Abide in Him. Let the Spirit flow. The fruit will come. It always does when the branch stays connected to the Vine.

✅Weekend Practice
This Sabbath, before you do anything else in the morning, sit quietly for five minutes and answer one question honestly in writing (on paper, not a screen): "Right now, today, is my relationship with God more like a connected branch producing fruit, or a branch that has been running on reserve? What is one thing blocking the door?" Do not judge yourself. Just be honest. Then pray the simplest prayer of the week: "Father, give me Your Holy Spirit today. I want to remain in the Vine." Carry that prayer into every conversation, every Sabbath moment, and every quiet space this weekend creates.
🙏Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, this week You held up a mirror and asked us to look. Some of what we saw was hard to face. We found Laodicean comfort where we expected vibrant faith. We found dry branches where we hoped to see fruit. But You did not show us our condition to shame us. You showed us so we could come home. Thank You for Your everlasting love that preceded every prayer we have ever prayed. Thank You for the true Vine, Jesus, who was crushed so that His life could flow into ours. Thank You for the Holy Spirit, the sap that makes dead things live again. We open the door. We remain. We receive. Do in us this week what we could never do on our own: produce the fruit that only comes from being connected to You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
