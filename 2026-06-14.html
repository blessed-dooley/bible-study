Out of the Overflow

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 12, Share Him Part 2 of 7
June 14, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Eleven men climb a hill in Galilee, and when they see the risen Jesus, some worship and some still wrestle with doubt. He commissions that exact group anyway: all authority, all nations, everything I commanded, with you always. Four "alls" wrapped around eleven ordinary men. Weeks later, two fishermen stand before the most educated council in Jerusalem, and the only explanation anyone can find for their boldness is that "These men had been with Jesus." Witnessing is not a performance for experts. It is telling what you have seen Him do, and it runs on being with Him.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday a Roman-era philosopher sneered at the wool-workers and cobblers who were talking about Jesus in kitchens and shops, and we said he had accidentally written down the church's secret. We also heard the Servant of Isaiah 50:4 describe where words for the weary actually come from: an ear God wakes morning by morning, long before the tongue opens. Today we climb the hill where that whole movement started. The risen Jesus is about to hand the family business to eleven ordinary men, and what He says to them, and what it turned them into, answers the question most of us are quietly asking: who am I to share Him?

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Students Who Never Made the Cut

A Jewish boy in first-century Galilee started memorizing Torah around age five. By ten, the best students moved on to the oral traditions; by fifteen, only the sharpest few were still standing, and the very best of those would seek out a famous rabbi and beg to follow him. Everyone else went home to the family trade. That was the system. By the time you met a grown fisherman mending nets on the Sea of Galilee, you were looking at a man the system had already passed over. He had not made the cut, and everyone in his village knew it.

That is who Jesus chose. When the Jerusalem council sizes up Peter and John, Luke records that they perceived them to be "unschooled, ordinary men," which was simply true by the schools' standards. No rabbinic training, no credentials, Galilean accents. Yet these were the men out-teaching the scholars in the temple courts, and the council's own minutes record the only explanation available: These men had been with Jesus. The rabbi who took the leftover students turned out to be the one teacher whose classroom changes what a person is made of. That detail is not trivia. It is the whole hope held out to every one of us who never went to seminary.

📖 Into the Word

Picture the hillside in Galilee where the risen Jesus told the eleven to meet Him. Matthew is honest enough to record that even there, with the resurrection standing in front of them, "some doubted." Jesus gives the Great Commission of Matthew 28:18-20 to that mixed group anyway, and the question worth sitting with is what exactly He handed them. The scenes from Acts show us the commission running live through ordinary men, and John's letter names the fuel the whole engine runs on. None of it looks like a sales pitch. All of it looks like overflow.

Matthew 28:18-20
☩ Open in Bible App
18Then Jesus came to them and said, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me. 19Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, 20and teaching them to obey all that I have commanded you. And surely I am with you always, even to the end of the age.”

Read it again and count the word "all." All authority. All nations. Everything I commanded. Always. In Greek it is the same little word each time, pas, meaning all, every, the whole of it, and Jesus hangs the entire Commission on it like a frame. The command in the middle ("make disciples") is real, but notice what it is wrapped in: total authority before it and unbroken presence after it. We are not sent out to win the world on our own steam. We are sent inside Someone else's authority and alongside Someone else's presence. This is the same everlasting gospel that flies to "every nation, tribe, language, and people" in Revelation 14:6-12, the final invitation before He returns. The job is enormous. The "alls" are bigger.

Acts 1:8
☩ Open in Bible App
But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.”

Notice what Jesus does not say. He does not say "you will be My lawyers" or "you will be My salesmen." He says witnesses. A witness in a courtroom has exactly one job, to tell what they personally saw and heard, and that job description rescues witnessing from everything we fear about it. You do not need to win the argument. You do not need to answer every objection. You need to tell what happened to you. And the power for even that much does not come from personality or training. It comes when the Holy Spirit comes. The geography ripples outward like a stone dropped in water, from the city they were standing in to the ends of the earth, but it always starts the same way. Spirit first. Then speech.

Acts 4:13
☩ Open in Bible App
When they saw the boldness of Peter and John and realized that they were unschooled, ordinary men, they marveled and took note that these men had been with Jesus.

This is the council that condemned Jesus, now staring at two of His fishermen. They run the credentials check and the men fail it: no schooling, no standing, nothing. And then comes the sentence that should be framed on every believer's wall. They recognized that These men had been with Jesus. Time spent with Him had become visible. It showed in their boldness, their words, their refusal to be rattled by the most intimidating room in the nation. When the council orders them to stop, Peter and John answer that they are "unable to stop speaking about what we have seen and heard" (Acts 4:20). Not unwilling. Unable. That is what overflow sounds like under pressure.

1 John 4:7-11
☩ Open in Bible App
7Beloved, let us love one another, because love comes from God. Everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God. 8Whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love. 9This is how God’s love was revealed among us: God sent His one and only Son into the world, so that we might live through Him. 10And love consists in this: not that we loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins. 11Beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another.

Here is the fuel line. John, the other fisherman from that courtroom, writes decades later as an old man, and he has boiled everything down to this. Love is not one of God's moods. God is where love comes from at all. And the love that sends a believer across the room to a hurting coworker is not self-generated niceness. It is a response. He loved us first, all the way to the cross, and people who have actually received that love start leaking it. This is why witnessing can never really be faked or forced. The early church did not love people in order to convert them. They loved people because they had been loved, and conversions followed the way a harvest follows rain.

🎯 Street Level
The Camera Roll You Didn't Ask For

Ask a brand-new grandmother one polite question about the baby. One. Watch what happens. The phone is already out, and you are suddenly forty photos deep into a camera roll: the hospital hat, the first bath, the yawn you apparently have to see. Nobody trained her for this. There is no weekend seminar called How to Talk About Your Grandchild. She has no notes, no script, and no fear that you might ask a question she cannot answer. She is not trying to convince you of anything. She is just so full of someone she loves that the overflow does the talking.

Here is the strange part. That same grandmother will tell you, with complete sincerity, that she could never share her faith because she "wouldn't know what to say." But witnessing was never a presentation. It is the camera-roll reflex pointed at Jesus. Peter and John had no credentials and no script either. What they had was time with Him, so much of it that a hostile courtroom could see it on them. You do not need training to talk about someone you love. You need the love. Spend the time with Jesus, and the overflow will find its own words.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
Trust Moved Next Door

In January 2026, the global communications firm Edelman released its twenty-sixth annual Trust Barometer, a survey of nearly 34,000 people across 28 countries. The headline was bleak. Trust in institutional voices keeps draining away: over the past five years, national government leaders have lost sixteen points of trust and major news organizations have lost eleven. But the report found that trust did not simply evaporate. It moved. The biggest gains went to the people closest to us, with "my neighbors, family and friends" and "my coworkers" each up eleven points over the same stretch. The researchers describe a world pulling its trust out of distant institutions and placing it in small, familiar circles, the faces people actually know.

Now reread that courtroom scene in Acts 4. The council was the institution, the most credentialed voice in the nation, and it had already lost the room. The men people believed were two uneducated fishermen whose only qualification was that "These men had been with Jesus." Twenty centuries before the survey, God built His entire communication plan on the exact channel the data says still works. The most persuasive voice about Jesus that your coworker will hear this year will not be a broadcast or a billboard. It will be a person they already know, telling what they have seen. The method has not aged, because it was never a method. It is how love travels.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Matthew's Gospel opens with a baby named Immanuel, "God with us" (Matthew 1:23), and closes on this hillside with the same promise in the first person: "I am with you always, even to the end of the age." The entire book is wrapped in His presence the way the Commission is wrapped in His "alls." That matters for witnessing more than any technique ever could, because the command was never "go for Me" but "go with Me." The Christ who sends you to your neighbor arrives at your neighbor's door at the same moment you do. He did the same thing Himself: He did not shout the gospel from heaven, He moved into the neighborhood, ate at the tables, touched the lepers, and told the truth in person. Every awkward, stumbling, ordinary act of sharing Him is carried by a Savior who has already gone ahead. We never witness alone. Not once. Not ever.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Today, write down one specific thing you have personally seen God do in your life in the past year: an answered prayer, a peace that did not make sense, a provision that arrived on time, a habit He has been breaking. Two or three sentences, in plain words, the way a witness would say it. Then share it with one person before the day ends. A text message counts. Do not explain doctrine. Just tell what you saw.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, we have spent years telling ourselves we are not qualified to share You. Today You showed us a hillside full of ordinary men, some of them still doubting, and You commissioned them anyway. Thank You that the Commission rests on Your authority and not our ability, on Your presence and not our polish. Give us what You gave Peter and John: not eloquence, but so much time with Jesus that it becomes visible. Fill us with the love John wrote about, the love that moved first, the love that sent Your Son, until it overflows onto the people You have placed in our path. And when the moment comes to speak, remind us that we are witnesses, not lawyers. We only have to tell what we have seen. In Jesus' name, Amen.
