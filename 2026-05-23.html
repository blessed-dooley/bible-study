Sin, the Gospel, and the Law

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 9, Sin, the Gospel, and the Law Part 1 of 7

✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

A man under siege sings the longest song in the Bible about a list of rules, and what he calls those rules is the only thing that has kept him alive.

  • ☩Sin is not a footnote in the Bible. It is the wound everything else is treating, and the Memory Text comes from a man saying God's precepts are why he is still breathing.
  • ☩Samson with his hair in his lap, Jesus saying cut off the hand that traps you, Paul writing that the law was always meant to show us our own face.

  • ☩By Thursday Jesus is on a Galilean hillside teaching how to build a house the storm cannot move, and the difference between the two builders is doing, not knowing.
🔁 Connection to Last Week

Last week ended on Revelation 14:12 and the four words at the back of the verse, ten pistin Iesou, the faith of Jesus. We held that phrase like a coin in our pocket all week. But that verse has six words at the front of it we have not yet read together. The saints "keep the commands of God." Last week's faith does not sit alone in that sentence. It sits next to the law. So this week opens the other half of the pair we already know the second half of. What does it mean to keep the commands of God when the only people who can read them accurately are the ones standing under the gospel that has already saved them? The week before us is the law and the gospel held in the same hand.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Words for the Wound

English has one short word for what the Bible has six or seven. Open the Hebrew side and the noun for sin is not a single tile, it is a small cabinet of related terms. Chattat is missing the mark, the same word Judges 20:16 uses for an army of left-handed Benjamites who could sling a stone at a hair and never miss. The Greek translators carried it into the New Testament as hamartia, the same archery image. Pesha is a different word for a different kind of failure, the deliberate cracking of a sworn relationship, what we would call covenant breach. Avon is bent or twisted, the moral shape of a beam that should be straight. In one breath Exodus 34 puts all three of them in God's own mouth as the things He forgives. The Bible refuses to use a single flat English-sized word for them, because the Bible refuses to flatten what sin actually does.

Standing alongside that vocabulary is a list the Hebrew Bible calls by four different words of its own: piqqudim, mitzvot, huqqim, torah, the precepts, commands, statutes, and instruction. The longest psalm in the Bible (Psalm 119, one hundred seventy-six verses, an acrostic running from aleph to tav, the alphabet anchors that organize each of its stanzas) is one sustained love song to that list. Verses 93 and 94 of that psalm are this week's Memory Text. They were written by a man under threat, and his confession is the answer to the question this week is going to keep asking. The precepts are not a burden God added to make the relationship harder. They are the floor under his feet on a day he expected to fall through the boards.

📖 This Week's Memory Text
Psalms 119:93-94
☩ Open in Bible App
93I will never forget Your precepts, for by them You have revived me. 94I am Yours; save me, for I have sought Your precepts.

Two short verses out of one hundred seventy-six, and they carry the whole logic of the week. "Your precepts" is one word in Hebrew, piqqudim, and it means something close to "things You have placed in my charge," a deposit entrusted to a person who is expected to keep watch over it. The verb behind "have revived me" is chayah, the Hebrew word for being brought back to life, restored, kept alive when death was already in the room. We met it six weeks ago at Isaiah 57:15, where the same God revives the spirit of the lowly. The psalmist is not saying God's precepts are intellectually interesting. He is saying they are the reason his lungs still move. The verb behind "sought" or "studied" is darash, the same diligent inquiry word the psalm reaches for again and again, the language of a person digging because the dig is worth his life.

This is the man whose pen sings the song the rest of the week is going to weigh. Six daily texts have been laid out around his confession. Judges 14 hands us Samson, the man who knew the precepts and still walked into the riddle, the wedding, the lion, and the woman who took the secret out of his mouth. Mark 9:42-48 hands us Jesus speaking with the urgency of a surgeon: cut off the hand that traps you. Romans 3:20 hands us Paul saying that the law was never the cure but the diagnosis, the lamp God hung in the room so we could see what was already there. Matthew 5:17-18 hands us Jesus on the side of a hill insisting that not one jot or tittle of the law will drop out of the story. Romans 3:28 hands us Paul announcing the rescue that the law itself could never give. Matthew 7:24-29 closes the week with two builders, two foundations, and one storm that comes for both.

Notice that the Memory Text already carries both halves of what is coming. "Your precepts" and "save me" sit inside the same breath of the same psalmist. He is not picking between them. He is leaning on both at once. The law has kept him alive. The Lord he calls Owner is the One who will save him. This week is going to keep proving that those two sentences belong on the same page. The wound is real, the lamp is needed, and the Surgeon has already arrived. Isaiah 59:2 names the wound clearly (our sins have built a wall between us and God), and the cross is the place that wall came down. By Wednesday it will be obvious that the law and the gospel were never two competing systems but two faces of one God, and by Thursday Jesus is going to ask whether we are not just hearing the words but doing them, the way a builder digs to bedrock before he ever pours a foundation.

✝️ The Christ Connection

By Thursday this week we will be on the southern slope of the Mount of Beatitudes, watching Jesus close the Sermon on the Mount with one of His sharpest images. Two men, two houses, one storm. The wise builder is the one who hears His words and does them. The same Voice that spoke the Ten Commandments at Sinai is speaking from the hill in Galilee, and the connection is not accidental. Jesus is the One in whose mouth the law was never a weight. It was His Father's character spoken out loud. By Wednesday's pairing of law and gospel we will see the Cross is the place where the law's verdict is honored down to the last jot and tittle and where mercy walks free at the same time. The same Jesus who said "I have not come to abolish but to fulfill" is the One who came to do for us what we could not do for ourselves, so the precepts the psalmist sings would not just be his praise but our life. The week is going to keep zooming in. By Friday it will be clear that the law and the gospel were never two competing systems. They were always two faces of one God.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Take an index card or sticky note and write Psalm 119:93-94 on it in your own handwriting (the BSB passage we just read). Tape it where your eyes will land on it twice today and at least three times tomorrow before Sunday opens (the bathroom mirror, the dashboard, the kitchen counter, the inside of your wallet). Each time you read it, ask one question: "What part of God's precepts has kept me alive this year that I have stopped thanking Him for?" Carry the card into the rest of the week. The next six days are going to weigh the law and the gospel together, and this verse is the heartbeat underneath the whole conversation.
🙏 Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we come into this week with hearts that have been quietly shrugging at sin and have not even noticed we were doing it. The world around us has gotten so comfortable with our brokenness that we have started to file Yours as one preference among many. Forgive us. Show us this week that sin is not a footnote You can edit out of the story. It is the wound everything else is treating. Teach us to read Your law the way the psalmist read it, not as a fence keeping us small but as the floor under our feet on a day we expected to fall through. And when by Wednesday we are standing in front of the verdict Your law honestly delivers, walk us straight into the gospel Your Son already finished for us. Help us hear, by Thursday, the Sermon on the Mount the way the first hearers heard it. Not as a higher set of rules to fail at, but as the family resemblance growing in people who have already been rescued. We do not need a better edit of the Bible. We need You. In Jesus' name, Amen.

A relationship pattern you have stopped flinching at. A category of media you have stopped guarding the door on. A grudge you now describe as 'being honest.') What would it look like this week to let the law name it again and the gospel meet you exactly where the naming lands?"
