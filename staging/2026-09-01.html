Christ-focused Ministry of Reconciliation

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 10, Authentic Christian Ministry Part 4 of 7
September 1, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

In the ancient world everyone knew who sent the peace delegation. The losers did, walking out with olive branches to beg the victor's terms. Paul announces an embassy running the wrong direction. The offended King, the One with every right to demand tribute, sends His own ambassadors to the rebels and pleads with them to come home. Behind the plea stands a price already paid, the sinless One made sin so sinners could stand righteous, and a love that compels His messengers off dead center. The wronged God made the peace Himself, and He sends His people out carrying the plea.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday closed with a tentmaker holding the deed to a building from God, eyes fixed on the unseen, and the passage ended somewhere sobering. At the judgment seat of Christ, where every life renders its account. Today picks up in the very next breath. Since we know what it means to fear the Lord, Paul says, we persuade. Notice what accountability produces in him. Not paralysis, and not a scramble to look impressive. Motion. A man who knows the jar is temporary and the Judge is certain stops living for himself, and today we find out what that freed-up life is actually for.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Wronged King Sent the Peace Delegation

Picture the end of an ancient rebellion. The city that rose up has lost, and everyone inside its walls knows what happens next. The gates open, and out comes the procession nobody ever wanted to march in, city fathers stripped of their finery, olive branches in hand, walking slowly toward the victor's camp to beg for terms. That was how peace worked. The offending side sent the delegation. The offended side sat enthroned and decided whether to be merciful. Every treaty, every surrender, every reconciliation between powers ran on that one unquestioned rule, and no defeated city ever looked up to see the victor's own envoys walking toward them first with an offer of full pardon. Kings did not plead with rebels. Rebels pleaded with kings.

Hold that picture, and now listen to Paul. God was reconciling the world to Himself in Christ, not counting men's trespasses against them, and He has sent out ambassadors, as though God were making His appeal through us. Read it against the customs of the age and the sentence is scandalous. The offended Sovereign dispatches the embassy. The wronged party draws up terms of peace that cost Him everything and His rebels nothing. Heaven’s envoys do not arrive collecting tribute. They arrive imploring, begging the guilty to accept an amnesty already purchased. Corinth had watched embassies come and go for centuries. It had never seen one shaped like this, and neither has anybody since.

📖 Into the Word

Two forces can move a preacher's mouth, and Corinth knew the first one well. Money and applause. Paul names a different pair. He does his persuading with a judgment seat behind him and a love out in front of him, reverent awe before the Lord he answers to and the compelling love of the Christ who died for all. That combination produces something rare, ministry with the self carried out of the center, and it exists to deliver a message the world has never once produced on its own. The wronged party wants peace, and He has already paid for it. Watch the direction everything moves today.

2 Corinthians 5:11-15
☩ Open in Bible App
11Therefore, since we know what it means to fear the Lord, we try to persuade men. What we are is clear to God, and I hope it is clear to your conscience as well. 12We are not commending ourselves to you again. Instead, we are giving you an occasion to be proud of us, so that you can answer those who take pride in appearances rather than in the heart. 13If we are out of our mind, it is for God; if we are of sound mind, it is for you. 14For Christ’s love compels us, because we are convinced that One died for all, therefore all died. 15And He died for all, that those who live should no longer live for themselves, but for Him who died for them and was raised again.

Since we know what it means to fear the Lord, we persuade. The fear driving Paul is not terror of a tyrant. He has just said every life will render its account at the judgment seat of Christ (2 Corinthians 5:10), and what that certainty produces is reverence with its sleeves rolled up, the fear Israel was taught long ago, to walk in God's ways, to love Him, to serve Him with all your heart and soul (Deuteronomy 10:12). Awe like that persuades people. It does not perform for them (2 Corinthians 5:11). And under the awe runs the engine. Christ's love compels us, presses in on every side, because One died for all, and those who live no longer get to orbit themselves. They live for Him who died for them and was raised again. A ministry is Christ-focused when the self has been carried off the throne. That is what the Corinthians should measure.

Which is why Paul refuses to enter the commendation contest. He hands the church an occasion to be proud of him, ammunition against the teachers who take pride in appearances rather than the heart (2 Corinthians 5:12), but his own boasting runs one direction only. My grace is sufficient for you, the Lord told him, for My power is perfected in weakness, so he boasts in the weaknesses where Christ's power rests (2 Corinthians 12:9). Far be it from me to boast, he wrote elsewhere, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ (Galatians 6:14). And what exactly did that cross accomplish? Paul answers with the biggest peace treaty ever announced.

2 Corinthians 5:16-21
☩ Open in Bible App
16So from now on we regard no one according to the flesh. Although we once regarded Christ in this way, we do so no longer. 17Therefore if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away. Behold, the new has come! 18All this is from God, who reconciled us to Himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation: 19that God was reconciling the world to Himself in Christ, not counting men’s trespasses against them. And He has committed to us the message of reconciliation. 20Therefore we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making His appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ: Be reconciled to God. 21God made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that in Him we might become the righteousness of God.

Every verb of rescue in this passage belongs to God. Paul's word for it is katallassō, Greek for reconcile, at its root an exchange, the marketplace term for trading one currency for another, here the trade of hostility for friendship. God made the exchange Himself, reconciling us through Christ, and then did the unthinkable, He handed the ministry of it to the people He had just forgiven (2 Corinthians 5:18). Anyone in Christ is a new creation, the old gone, the new arrived (2 Corinthians 5:17). The world's trespasses are no longer being counted (2 Corinthians 5:19), and the King's envoys go out not to collect but to implore, be reconciled to God (2 Corinthians 5:20). Then verse 21 opens the treasury and shows what the peace cost. The sinless One was made sin so that sinners could stand as the righteousness of God. He absorbed the war. He funds the amnesty. We just carry the letter.

How far does a peace like that reach? Paul's letter to Colossae takes the same treaty and unrolls the map.

Colossians 1:19-23
☩ Open in Bible App
19For God was pleased to have all His fullness dwell in Him, 20and through Him to reconcile to Himself all things, whether things on earth or things in heaven, by making peace through the blood of His cross. 21Once you were alienated from God and were hostile in your minds, engaging in evil deeds. 22But now He has reconciled you by Christ’s physical body through death to present you holy, unblemished, and blameless in His presence— 23if indeed you continue in your faith, established and firm, not moved from the hope of the gospel you heard, which has been proclaimed to every creature under heaven, and of which I, Paul, have become a servant.

The scope is staggering. All the fullness of God was pleased to dwell in Christ, and through Him God set out to reconcile all things, things on earth and things in heaven, one rebellion-scarred creation brought back into harmony. And the means was not negotiation. Peace was made through the blood of His cross, which is strange treaty ink. Then Paul turns the telescope around and points it at the reader. You were the alienated ones, hostile in mind, enemies in your evil deeds, and now you stand reconciled by Christ's own physical body through death, presented holy and unblemished and blameless in His presence. The passage ends with one sober word, continue, stay rooted in the hope of this gospel. Peace this expensive is not a souvenir. It is ground to stand on.

And the treaty has one more clause, the one Corinth's divided house needed most. Peace with God tears down the walls people keep between each other.

Ephesians 2:13-16
☩ Open in Bible App
13But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far away have been brought near through the blood of Christ. 14For He Himself is our peace, who has made the two one and has torn down the dividing wall of hostility 15by abolishing in His flesh the law of commandments and decrees. He did this to create in Himself one new man out of the two, thus making peace 16and reconciling both of them to God in one body through the cross, by which He put to death their hostility.

Jews and Gentiles had a wall between them, custom on top of commandment on top of contempt, and everyone had learned to live with it. The cross did not learn to live with it. Christ Himself is our peace, Paul says, and in His flesh the dividing wall of hostility came down, two long-hostile peoples created into one new humanity, both reconciled to God in one body through the cross, the hostility itself put to death. Notice the architecture of the gospel here. The vertical peace and the horizontal peace are one piece of work, signed in the same blood. Nobody gets to keep a private treaty with God while maintaining a wall against the people He died to include. The embassy message was never only about your soul. It is about a new family where the war used to be.

🎯 Street Level
Your Library Card Still Works

Somewhere in your house, or your childhood, there is a library book you never returned. You know the one. It migrated under a bed, surfaced years later in a move, and by then the fine had grown teeth in your imagination, so you did the math shame always does. You stopped going. Not because you hated the library. Because you owed it. People have avoided that building for a decade over an amount smaller than a fast-food lunch, crossing the street away from a place full of things they loved, paying the debt the only way shame knows how to pay anything, with distance.

Which is why the sign so many libraries have started posting reads like a misprint. All fines forgiven. Come back. Think about who is saying that. The library is the wronged party in this story. It is still missing the book. Yet it absorbs the loss, zeroes the ledger at its own expense, and then goes out of its way to announce the amnesty, because it wants the reader back more than it wants the money. That is the direction of today's passage, the direction nobody expects. God was reconciling the world to Himself in Christ, not counting our trespasses against us, and He sends people out to say so, pleading, not collecting. All those years you spent crossing the street, the debt was already settled and the door already open. The lock was never on His side. It was the shame, and it was yours, and the sign is up. Come home.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Shared Fence Line After Genocide

In 1994, neighbors murdered neighbors in Rwanda, more than 800,000 people in about a hundred days. What happened after the prisons began releasing confessed perpetrators is an extraordinary post-genocide housing experiment. An organization called Prison Fellowship Rwanda developed an action-based reconciliation model, a network of eight reconciliation villages supporting roughly 830 people directly, where genocide survivors and the people who had carried out the killing agreed to live as next-door neighbors, farm cooperatively, and raise their children on the same lanes. A study published in May 2025 in the journal Discover Social Science and Health ran focus groups in five selected villages and the communities near them, and several of its authors are affiliated with Prison Fellowship Rwanda, which supported the data collection. What residents described was a process, not a shortcut. Structured dialogue and shared labor, trust slowly rebuilt, trauma releasing its grip, offenders repenting, confessing, and asking forgiveness, and survivors freely choosing to grant it.

Every instinct says a wrong that size should end the story for good. What the villages show is how much it takes for the story to continue. Structured peacebuilding brought offenders and survivors to the same table, the offenders named what they had done, confessed it, and asked for forgiveness, the survivors chose, freely, to grant it, and then the community kept talking and kept working the same fields until trust had somewhere to live. One survivor in the study called living there God's purpose. The shape of it should look familiar. God was reconciling the world to Himself in Christ, not counting men's trespasses against them, the dividing wall of hostility torn down in His own flesh. Rwanda's villages are not the gospel, and nobody in them pretends forgiveness came cheap. But they are what a street looks like when reconciliation does what reconciliation always does, names the wrong out loud, offers grace that costs the one who gives it, and builds shared life where hostility used to hold the ground. Where that happens, the war actually ends, and the watching world leans in to ask how.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Stand at the center of today's passage and look up. God made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that in Him we might become the righteousness of God. Theologians call it the great exchange, and it is the only reason the embassy exists. Christ is not merely the messenger of the peace. He is the peace, in person, which is exactly how Paul puts it to the Ephesians. He Himself is our peace. The King did not draft terms from a safe throne room and send them down. He came in person, stood on the rebels' soil, and let the war's full violence land on Himself, the sinless One absorbing the sentence so the guilty could inherit the verdict He deserved. When His ambassadors say be reconciled to God, they are not offering a negotiation. They are announcing an accomplished fact with a signature on it, written at Calvary in His own blood. The wronged One moved first. He always had to be the One, because only He could pay it.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Run the embassy in the direction God runs it. Today, think of the one relationship where you are the wronged party, the one where you are owed the apology, the first call, the acknowledgment. Make the first move anyway. One honest text, one call, one invitation to coffee, no scorekeeping and no speech about what they did. You are not declaring them right. You are declaring the war over on your side, the way Someone once declared it over on His.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, we have been reading about ourselves today, and we know it. We were the rebel city. We were the alienated and the hostile in mind, and while we were still holding out, You sent Your peace delegation, and then You came Yourself, and the terms were written in the blood of Your Son. Thank You that our trespasses are not being counted. Thank You that in Christ we stand as new creations, holy and unblemished in Your presence, a verdict we could never have bargained for. Now do in us what You did in Paul. Let the fear of You make us diligent and the love of Christ make us move. Show us the people who are still outside the peace, and put Your appeal in our mouths without one note of superiority, because we are only beggars who found the amnesty. And where we have been the offended ones, nursing a righteous grievance, give us the courage to move first. We want to be ambassadors who resemble our King. In Jesus' name, Amen.
