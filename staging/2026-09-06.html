The Example of Jesus

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 11, Stewardship and Mission Part 2 of 7
September 6, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Paul has barely finished making peace with Corinth when he raises the awkward item left on the agenda, a relief pledge that stalled while they fought. He restarts it with news from the north. Macedonia's churches, broke and battered, have been begging for the chance to give. Then he plays the highest card he holds, one verse of gospel told in money language. Jesus was rich. He became poor, on purpose, for you. His poverty is the only reason you are rich. Every real gift is a small echo of the richest Giver choosing poverty for love.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday ended with a slip of paper in your hand and a strange promise, that two chapters about an offering would keep turning into a mirror. We watched the project itself take shape, coins gathered week by week and carried by hand across eight hundred dangerous miles, toward believers on the far side of the world's oldest religious wall. Today the letter opens that project's file in Corinth, and it is awkward reading. This church started the fund a year ago, then let it stall while they fought with Paul. Watch how he restarts a stalled grace. Not with a lecture on obligation. With an example, and behind the example, the Example.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Gift Came Out of a Province That Had Nothing

Paul carried a specific memory of Macedonia on his own body. Philippi, after midnight, his back split open by Roman rods, his feet locked in stocks, singing anyway. The jailer who washed those wounds before dawn became a believer the same night, and a church was born inside a prison's blast radius. Thessalonica followed, birthed in the middle of a city riot, and Berea's new believers watched agitators arrive by road to break up what God was building. That is the soil the Macedonian churches grew in. Pressure from the day they opened their doors, and, in a province that had been fought over and stripped by Rome's wars for generations, precious little money.

So when Paul writes that in a terrible ordeal their abundant joy and deep poverty overflowed into rich generosity, he is not reciting a fundraising legend. He is describing people he knew by name, people whose giving came out of bread money, not surplus. Now feel the pressure of the comparison landing on Corinth. The wealthy church in the famous port had made the first pledge and then let it die in a drawer. The broke church up north was pleading for the privilege of giving more. The example marching ahead of Corinth this morning is not impressive because of its size. It is impressive because of its cost, and cost is exactly where today's verse is headed.

📖 Into the Word

Every reconciliation eventually reaches the moment when the old unfinished business comes back up. Paul has spent seven chapters repairing what broke between him and Corinth (2 Corinthians 1:1-7:16), and now, with the friendship standing again, he turns to the item everyone knew was still on the table, the two chapters Paul now devotes to the collection (2 Corinthians 8:1-9:15). A year-old promise to the poor. The question is how anyone restarts a stalled generosity without crushing it, and Paul's answer begins with what this fund actually was.

1 Corinthians 16:1-4
☩ Open in Bible App
1Now about the collection for the saints, you are to do as I directed the churches of Galatia: 2On the first day of every week, each of you should set aside a portion of his income, saving it up, so that when I come no collections will be needed. 3Then, on my arrival, I will send letters with those you recommend to carry your gift to Jerusalem. 4And if it is advisable for me to go also, they can travel with me.

This is the fund's founding document, written to Corinth about a year earlier, and notice how unglamorous it is. A little set aside on the first day of every week, by each person, saved up quietly so that nobody has to be squeezed when Paul arrives. Couriers the church chooses itself, letters of accountability, Jerusalem as the destination. The Corinthians had responded eagerly. They were the first not only to give but even to want to (2 Corinthians 8:10-11), and Paul had promised to send brothers ahead so the gift could be prepared generously instead of begrudgingly (2 Corinthians 9:5). Then the relationship ruptured, and the weekly rhythm died with it. A grace, it turns out, can stall. That is the file sitting open on Paul's desk.

How do you restart it? Paul does not reach for Corinth's guilt. He reaches for Macedonia's joy, and he tells the story as if reporting a miracle, because to him it is one.

2 Corinthians 8:1-7
☩ Open in Bible App
1Now, brothers, we want you to know about the grace that God has given the churches of Macedonia. 2In the terrible ordeal they suffered, their abundant joy and deep poverty overflowed into rich generosity. 3For I testify that they gave according to their ability and even beyond it. Of their own accord, 4they earnestly pleaded with us for the privilege of sharing in this service to the saints. 5And not only did they do as we expected, but they gave themselves first to the Lord and then to us, through the will of God. 6So we urged Titus to help complete your act of grace, just as he had started it. 7But just as you excel in everything—in faith, in speech, in knowledge, in complete earnestness, and in the love we inspired in you—see that you also excel in this grace of giving.

Look where the story starts. Not with what Macedonia gave, but with what God gave Macedonia. Grace lands first, and generosity is what it looks like coming out the other side. Then the math turns impossible on purpose. Ordeal plus deep poverty produced overflowing generosity (2 Corinthians 8:2), beyond their ability, unprompted, and the begging in the scene runs backward. Nobody is begging them to give. They are begging Paul for the privilege. Verse 5 quietly hands over the secret, and it is the sentence to carry all week. They gave themselves first to the Lord. Money was just what followed the surrender out of the wallet. So Paul turns to rich, gifted Corinth with an invitation, not an invoice. You excel in faith, in speech, in knowledge. Excel in this grace too.

Then Paul stops the appeal cold to clarify what he is doing. He is not commanding, he says. He is testing the sincerity of their love against the earnestness of others (2 Corinthians 8:8-15), because Macedonia was never the real standard. The example behind every example is next, and Paul spends it in a single verse.

2 Corinthians 8:9
☩ Open in Bible App
For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sake He became poor, so that you through His poverty might become rich.

The whole story of redemption in one sentence, told in financial language. He was rich, and Scripture means that literally. Before the world existed, the Son shared the Father's own glory (John 17:5). He became poor, and Scripture means that literally too. The Son of Man had nowhere to lay His head (Luke 9:58). Paul's word for it is ptōcheia, Greek for beggar-poverty, the kind that owns nothing and has to ask. The route down ran through emptying Himself to take a servant's form (Philippians 2:7) and obedience all the way to a cross (Philippians 2:8). And the purpose clause aims the whole descent at you. So that you, through His poverty, might become rich. Heaven's wealth changed hands at Calvary. That is why Paul never needs to invoice anybody. He just shows them the receipt.

🎯 Street Level
The Empty Case on the Top Shelf

In the back of your parents' closet, on the shelf above the coats, sat a screw-down case with one baseball card inside, the card your dad had owned since he was nine. He never called it an investment. Everyone just knew the rules. You could look. You could not touch. It survived four moves, two garage sales, and every lean Christmas, the one possession he would have carried out of a fire. Then years later, home for a weekend, you go hunting on that shelf for a photo album and stop cold. The case is sitting in its old spot, latched, dusted, and empty. When you finally ask, your mom answers quietly. The empty case is exactly as old as your first tuition bill.

Notice what you learned in that hallway, and in what order. You always knew what you had received. The tuition got paid and your life went on ahead. What you did not know was what it cost, and the empty case told you, because the true measure of a gift was never the number on the bill. It was the treasure that left the shelf. That is exactly how Paul talks about Jesus. Though He was rich, yet for your sake He became poor. The descent was not bad luck. It was a decision, made by the only One rich enough to make it, and it was aimed, for your sake, at somebody with a name, you. The wealth you live on, forgiveness, belonging, a future, all of it corresponds to an emptiness He chose. This is why generosity never has to be extracted from people who have seen inside the case. You do not haggle with a love like that. You inherit it, and then you start giving like the family you come from.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
He Gave His Fortune Twenty Years to Live

On May 8, 2025, Bill Gates published an essay with a sentence you do not expect from a billionaire. "People will say a lot of things about me when I die," he wrote, "but I am determined that 'he died rich' will not be one of them." The man who spent decades at or near the top of the world's rich lists announced that virtually all of his remaining wealth will move through the Gates Foundation over the next twenty years, and that the foundation, drawing on its existing endowment along with everything he still plans to add, expects to spend more than two hundred billion dollars before it closes its doors for good on December 31, 2045. The money already has assignments. Ending the preventable deaths of mothers and babies. Finishing off ancient killers like polio and Guinea worm disease. He even borrowed his reasoning from Andrew Carnegie, who warned that "the man who dies thus rich dies disgraced."

A deadline like that rearranges the usual math. For once, a fortune is being measured by how fast it can leave, aimed at children who will never know the giver's name and could never repay him. That is the direction of travel in the verse Corinth heard, and it is worth saying plainly where the comparison runs out. Gates is scheduling a descent from unimaginable wealth to ordinary comfort. Jesus went to the bottom. He did not give a percentage, and what He handed over was not an endowment but Himself, down to a borrowed tomb. So let the modern headline be what it is, an echo that honors the original. When the richest man of an age decides that dying rich would disgrace him, he is agreeing, from a long way off, with the Lord who was rich and refused to stay that way while we were poor.

✝️ The Christ Connection

The danger on a day like this is admiring the example so long that we miss the purchase inside it. Second Corinthians 8:9 is not a poster about simple living. It is a receipt. He was rich describes eternity past, glory shared with the Father before anything was made. He became poor is a manger, a borrowed pillow, a borrowed tomb, the exact bottom of human need. But the purpose clause is where the gospel lives. For your sake. So that you might become rich. He did not become poor to make a point. He became poor to make a payment, and the goods were delivered, forgiveness, adoption, a future with no death in it. And poverty was not the last word. The One who emptied Himself is rich again in glory, and heaven's strange accounting has Him refusing to be rich alone. His people inherit with Him. He has never once regretted the price. The question He leaves with us is quieter. Now that you know the grace, what will knowing it do?

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Sometime today, give something away at actual cost to yourself, not out of surplus. Skip the thing you were going to buy for yourself this week and give that exact amount to someone in need, or hand your one free evening to a person who cannot pay you back. One condition. Keep it secret. Gifts that echo Jesus do not issue press releases. Tonight, tell Him honestly what it cost you, and then thank Him for what His gift cost Him.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, today we read Your story told in money language, and we hardly know what to do with it. You were rich in a way we cannot imagine, and You chose to become poor in a way we work our whole lives to avoid. You did it for our sake, with our names inside the purpose clause, and everything we have that matters, forgiveness, belonging, hope, a future, arrived through Your poverty. Forgive us for how casually we say thank You for that. Forgive us too for the pledges we made in an eager season and quietly let stall. We do not want to admire You from a distance today. We want to follow You downhill. Show each of us one act of giving that actually costs something, and give us the joy Macedonia had when we do it. We give ourselves first to You, again, right now. In Jesus' name, Amen.
