Spiritual Gifts

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 6, Spiritual Gifts Part 1 of 7
August 1, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

The sun is setting over a borrowed living room in Corinth. A dock worker settles onto the floor beside the man who owns the house, and everyone has brought something. A hymn. A teaching. A prayer in a language nobody in the room can follow. The place hums with gifts, and underneath the hum runs the old Corinthian itch to rank them. Into that room Paul sends three chapters and one command. Chase love first, then welcome every gift the Spirit gives. The gifts were never trophies to compare. They are body parts, and they only live connected.

🔁 Connection to Last Week

Last week ended in front of the last working altar in the modern world, the one with a mirror over it, and we watched the self lose its throne the way stars fade at sunrise. The compass did that work. Do everything for the glory of God, whether you eat or drink or whatever you do. This week Paul opens his hands and shows us what the whatever contains. Abilities. Capacities. Gifts. If even lunch can carry His glory, then the things God has made us good at certainly can. The purchased life turns out to be an equipped life.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
Church Was Somebody's Living Room

For the first two hundred years of Christianity there was no such thing as a church building. When the believers in Corinth came together, they walked through somebody's front door. A member with a house large enough, likely one of the few wealthy families in the congregation, opened it, and the church filled whatever space there was. The dining room held maybe nine people on its couches. Everyone else packed the courtyard and spilled into side rooms, a dock worker's shoulder against a city official's toga, a slave sitting in plain view of her owner. There was no stage, no platform, no printed order of service. Worship happened at floor level, face to face, in the room where the family had eaten breakfast that morning.

Now listen to that room. One person starts a hymn. Another has a teaching ready. Someone stands up with a burst of praise in a language nobody present can follow, and three more people are still waiting their turn. Every voice carries something real, and every voice is competing with the city's oldest habit, the itch to rank everything and everyone. We watched that itch split this church into fan clubs in the letter's opening chapters. Gathered worship in Corinth had all the makings of a glorious mess. Paul writes this week's chapters straight into that living room, and his answer is not a seating chart. It is a body.

📖 This Week's Memory Text
1 Corinthians 14:1
☩ Open in Bible App
Earnestly pursue love and eagerly desire spiritual gifts, especially the gift of prophecy.

Two verbs open this verse, and they are not the same size. Love gets the violent one. The Greek word diōkō means to chase something down, to hunt it, and it is the same word Scripture uses for persecution. Paul, the man who once hunted believers from house to house, now tells believers to hunt love with that same relentlessness. The gifts get a warmer verb. Desire them, welcome them, want what the Spirit wants to give. The order is the argument. Chase love first and the gifts become tools for building people up. Reverse the order and the gifts become trophies for measuring people against each other. Paul writes this line in the middle of a long section on what happens when the church gathers (1 Corinthians 11-14), and this week walks the heart of it (1 Corinthians 12-14).

The rest of the week unpacks that one breath. The church is a body with many parts and one Head, no part optional (1 Corinthians 12). The ascended Christ Himself stocked His church with apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, and teachers, gifts with faces, given for one job (Ephesians 4:11-13), the building up of the body of Christ (Ephesians 4:12). Between the gift chapters sits the greatest thing ever written on love (1 Corinthians 13), placed there on purpose. Peter adds that every gift is borrowed property, varied grace to be spent serving others as good stewards (1 Peter 4:8-11). Even the meeting itself gets instructions, two or three at most, each in turn (1 Corinthians 14:27), because the God who reveals His counsel to His servants the prophets (Amos 3:7) is a God of order, not noise. And the verse's last clause, especially that you may prophesy, waits for Thursday.

🗓️ This Week's Road Map
  • ☩Sunday: Varieties of Gifts. One Spirit hands out many different gifts, and the first of them is the nerve to say Jesus is Lord out loud.
  • ☩Monday: Unity Through Diversity. The church is a body, one and many at the same time, and the parts that look weakest turn out to be necessary.
  • ☩Tuesday: A More Excellent Way. Between two chapters about gifts, Paul stops everything to describe the one thing that makes any gift worth having.
  • ☩Wednesday: The Gift of Tongues. A real gift gets loud in Corinth, and Paul insists that words nobody understands build nobody up.
  • ☩Thursday: The Gift of Prophecy. The gift that speaks for God exists to strengthen and comfort, and every voice claiming it gets tested.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought & Discussion. The week lands on a body with no spare parts and love as the only power source.
✝️ The Christ Connection

Here is the seed to carry into the week. Every gift on the coming pages has a Giver, and He is not anonymous. When Christ ascended, He did not leave a memory behind. He left provision. The risen Jesus looked at His young church, knew exactly what it would face in cities like Corinth, and stocked it Himself, the way a builder stocks a job site before the crew ever arrives. He took His victory and handed out its spoils as gifts to people (Ephesians 4:8-11). So the real question under every gift list is not which gift do I have. It is what kind of Lord equips His people this thoroughly. Watch for Him all week. He is the Head directing the body on Monday, the love being described line by line on Tuesday, the Word worth understanding on Wednesday, and the One every true prophet has ever pointed to on Thursday. By Friday the gifts will have led us where they always lead. To Him.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Before Sunday, take five minutes and name one ability God has put in your hands. Not a churchy one, necessarily. Cooking for a crowd. Fixing what breaks. Remembering names. Staying calm when everyone else is spinning. Write it down, and beside it write the name of one person, or one corner of your church, that ability could strengthen this week. Then pray this week's verse over the page. Chase love. Welcome the gift. You have just drafted your assignment for the next six days.
🙏 Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You for a week that asks what You have placed in our hands. We confess the two mistakes we keep making with gifts. Sometimes we rank them, wanting the visible ones, envying the platformed ones, quietly grading each other by wattage. Sometimes we bury them, folding our hands because what we carry seems too small to matter. Forgive us for both. Walk us through the body You built this week. Show each of us the part that is ours, and teach us to chase love so hard that every gift finds its target. Guard our congregations from turning worship into competition. Where someone among us has carried an unglamorous gift faithfully for years, help us see them this week and say so. And when we gather, make the room a building site, every voice raising the same house. We want the Giver even more than the gifts. In Jesus' name, Amen.
