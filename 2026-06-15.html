Unforced but With Power

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 12, Share Him Part 3 of 7
June 15, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Jesus looks out at a crowd and something twists in His gut. They look like sheep nobody is shepherding. That ache, not ambition, is what kept Him healing and teaching day after day. Jeremiah knew the same engine from the inside, a fire in his bones he was too tired to hold in. And yet the God who feels all of that has never once forced a soul. Not in Eden, not at the ark, not over Jerusalem. He feeds, heals, invites, and weeps, and leaves the door unbarred. God's love compels us from the inside, but it never forces anyone from the outside.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday we stood with the council in Jerusalem as they ran a credentials check on two fishermen and came up with only one explanation for their boldness: they had been with Jesus. Witnesses, we said, not lawyers. People who tell what they saw. But that raises two questions the moment you try to live it. What keeps a witness going when the telling gets exhausting? And where is the line between sharing the truth and pushing it on someone? Today the Bible answers both with the same word. Love is the fuel, and love is also the limit. The God who powers the witness is the God who refuses to force a single heart open.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Empire That Required Incense

In the year 250, a Roman citizen could be handed a small slip of papyrus called a libellus. It was a receipt, signed by witnesses, certifying that the holder had burned a pinch of incense to the emperor's gods. The emperor Decius had ordered every inhabitant of the empire to sacrifice publicly and carry the proof. Archaeologists have dug dozens of these certificates out of the Egyptian sand. That was how the most powerful state on earth handled religion. Belief was a matter of compliance, and compliance could be commanded, audited, and filed.

Hold that papyrus next to the Bible's story and the contrast is startling. The God with actual "all authority" posted one command in Eden and left the tree within reach. He had Noah build the ark in public for years and forced no one up the ramp. He let Israel walk away from the covenant and promised they would find Him whenever they searched with all their heart. The empire, with far less authority, compelled everything and produced incense receipts. God, with all of it, compels nothing and wants hearts. Whenever the church has forgotten that difference and reached for the empire's tools, pressure, threat, social force, it has produced exactly what Decius produced: paperwork instead of love.

📖 Into the Word

Matthew gives us one sentence that explains how Jesus kept going through the endless days of preaching, healing, and being needed: He saw the crowds and felt it in His gut. Matthew 9:36 anchors the day because everything else hangs on what moved Him. From there the texts trace the same energy through Jeremiah's burning bones, then watch it stop, every single time, at the door of the human will. A hen spreads her wings over a city that says no. Power and restraint, in the same heart.

Matthew 9:36
☩ Open in Bible App
When He saw the crowds, He was moved with compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd.

The Greek word behind "felt compassion" is splanchnizomai, and it literally means to be moved in your inward parts, your stomach, your gut. This is not polite sympathy from a distance. It is the physical ache you feel when you see a child lost in a parking lot. That ache is what drove Jesus' relentless labor, and Paul says the same force is meant to drive ours: "the love of Christ compels us" (2 Corinthians 5:14). Notice what the crowds looked like to Him. Not projects. Not numbers. Sheep without a shepherd, weary and scattered. When we look at the faces in our own crowded days and feel nothing, the problem is not our schedule. It is our eyes.

Jeremiah 20:9
☩ Open in Bible App
If I say, “I will not mention Him or speak any more in His name,” His message becomes a fire burning in my heart, shut up in my bones, and I become weary of holding it in, and I cannot prevail.

Jeremiah had every reason to quit. He had just been beaten and locked in stocks for preaching, and this verse is him trying to resign. He cannot do it. The word of God in him behaves like banked coals in his skeleton, and holding it in is more exhausting than letting it out. That is what witnessing feels like when it is real. It is not a quota you grudgingly fill. It is a fire you cannot comfortably contain. Peter and John said the same thing to the council, that they were unable to stop speaking about what they had seen and heard. If sharing God has come to feel like pure obligation, the honest first prayer is not "give me courage" but "Lord, relight the fire."

Matthew 23:37
☩ Open in Bible App
O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those sent to her, how often I have longed to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, but you were unwilling!

Here is the fire and the restraint in one cry. Jesus stands over the city that will kill Him within the week, and the image He reaches for is a mother hen spreading her wings in a barnyard fire. How often I wanted. But you were not willing. He does not say "so I will make you." The God who never forced Adam and Eve away from the tree (Genesis 2:16-17), never forced anyone onto the ark (Genesis 7:1), and never forced Israel to stay in covenant (Deuteronomy 4:29-31) will not start coercing now. Instead He met needs first, healing and feeding whole regions (Matthew 4:23-25), and then invited. The urge to pressure, corner, or punish someone into truth never comes from heaven. The disposition to force a conscience is the enemy's signature, not Christ's. And yet the wings stay spread. He never gives up on us.

2 Peter 3:18
☩ Open in Bible App
But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To Him be the glory both now and to the day of eternity. Amen.

Peter's last recorded sentence is a growth chart. Grace and knowledge, both of them growing, both of them centered on a Person. This is where the unforced power actually comes from in daily life, because nobody can leak what they are not full of. A believer who is growing, even slowly, has something fresh to tell. A believer who stopped growing years ago has only a stale story and a set of opinions, and people can tell the difference in about a minute. The overcomers in Revelation conquer the enemy "by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (Revelation 12:11). Your testimony is simply your growth, told out loud. Keep growing and the witnessing largely takes care of itself.

🎯 Street Level
The Bird Only Lands on a Still Hand

There is a trick to getting a wild chickadee to eat from your hand, and everyone who has pulled it off says the same thing. You stand near the feeder in the cold, sunflower seeds in your open palm, and you hold still. That is the entire technique. The bird sees the food from the branch and wants it badly, but it takes its time anyway, sizing you up, hopping two twigs closer, vanishing, coming back. You cannot rush a single second of it. And the one move guaranteed to end it forever is the one your muscles are begging to make. Close the hand. Grab. Do that once and no bird lands on you again. Nothing that ever settled on an open palm was forced there.

That open palm is God's posture in every scene we just read. He fills the hand first: bread for the hungry, healing for whole regions, kindness ahead of any sermon. And then He holds still. The tree in Eden stayed within reach. The ramp of the ark stayed open. Jerusalem got spread wings, not a cage. When we share Him with someone, the same physics apply. The moment they feel our fingers start to curl, the cornering question, the guilt trip, the scoreboard, they are back on the branch, and the trust takes years to rebuild. Love fills the hand, holds it open, and lets the landing belong to them.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Message That Pushes People Away

In October 2025, the Journal of Communication published a meta-analysis with a blunt title, "Words That Trigger." Researchers pooled 35 studies covering 10,658 people to answer one question about health messages. What happens when the wording gets forceful, all "you must," "you have to," "there is no excuse"? The answer was consistent across the whole pile of data. Controlling language backfires. Readers felt their freedom being grabbed, got angry, started arguing back in their heads, and walked away less likely to take the advice than people who were handed the same facts with their freedom intact. Scientists call the reflex psychological reactance. Human beings are built with an alarm that trips the moment someone reaches for the steering wheel of their will, even when the driver is right.

That alarm is not a design flaw. It is a clue about the Designer. The God who wired us to resist coercion is the same God who posted one command in Eden and left the tree in reach, and the same Jesus who spread His wings over a city and accepted its no. He never triggers the alarm, because He never grabs. He feeds, heals, invites, and waits with an open hand. So when we corner someone about faith, we are not just being un-Christlike. We are setting off the exact alarm God built to protect the freedom He refuses to violate. Force has never once made a believer. It only ever made paperwork.

✝️ The Christ Connection

The cross is where "unforced but with power" stops being a slogan and becomes a Friday afternoon. Jesus told Peter He could call twelve legions of angels, and then He let men with hammers stretch out His hands. All authority in heaven and on earth, and He used none of it to save Himself and none of it to compel a single onlooker. One thief mocked. The other believed. Both watched the same dying Savior, and He forced neither verdict. That is the heart of God under maximum pressure: power fully present, coercion fully absent, love fully spent. And here is the wonder Paul could never get over: this uncoercive cross turned out to be the most compelling force in human history. "The love of Christ compels us." Not drives us like cattle. Draws us, the way that hen's spread wings were always meant to draw the chicks. The cross does not push anyone. It pulls everyone who will look at it long enough.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Jesus met needs first and invited second. Today, pick one person in your life who is far from God and do something concrete for them with no agenda attached: bring the coffee they like, take a chore off their plate, check on the thing they mentioned last week. Do not steer the conversation to spiritual things. Let the kindness be the sermon today, and ask God to relight the fire in your bones so that when the door opens later, the words are already warm.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, give us Your eyes for the crowds we move through, the checkout lines and break rooms and bleachers full of people who look fine and are not. Let what moved Jesus move us, that ache for sheep without a shepherd. We confess that we have sometimes confused pressure with passion, and pushed when You were inviting. Forgive us for reaching for the empire's tools. Teach us Your way instead: meet the need, spread the wings, leave the door unbarred, and never give up. And Lord, where our fire has gone cold and witnessing has become a duty we avoid, do what You did for Jeremiah. Put Your word in our hearts like a fire shut up in our bones, until holding it in is harder than letting it out. In Jesus' name, Amen.
