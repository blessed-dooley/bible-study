Paul's Ministry in Corinth

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 1, Paul's Ministry in Corinth Part 1 of 7
June 27, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

A church can be a mess. Corinth was. It was a brash boomtown of self-made men, packed with idol-temples and lawsuits and bragging, and the little church Paul planted there caught nearly every disease the city carried. This week we meet the man who refused to leave, the city that tried to wear him down, and the one line that kept him preaching the night he wanted to quit, that God had many people in this city. A new quarter opens not with a perfect church but a real one, and with news worth holding onto: the gospel is not too weak for the worst place you know.

🔁 Connection to Last Week

Last quarter ended with our eyes lifted all the way to the end of the story, to the day we finally see Jesus face to face and are made like Him. That hope was never meant to float above ordinary life. It was meant to land in it. So a new quarter brings us back down to the ground, into a noisy port city and a young church that is anything but heavenly, to ask the next honest question. What does following Jesus actually look like when the people around you, and sometimes the person in the pew beside you, are a mess? For the next thirteen weeks we walk through two letters Paul wrote to the church at Corinth, letters that hold some of the deepest truths in all of Scripture. And we begin where he began, by remembering who he was and why he came.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
A City With No Grandparents

In 146 BC, Rome did to Corinth what it rarely did to anyone. It wiped the place off the map, burned the city, killed or sold its people, and left the ground empty for a hundred years. Then in 44 BC Julius Caesar built it back from nothing, and he did not repopulate it with old Greek nobility. He shipped in freed slaves, discharged soldiers, and traders from every corner of the empire and told them to start over. By the time Paul walked in, Corinth was barely a century old and already rich, straddling the narrow land bridge where cargo from two seas was hauled across rather than risked around the cape.

That history shaped the people in a particular way. Everyone there was new money. There were no founding families, no inherited pedigree, none of the quiet confidence that comes from simply belonging. What you had instead was a whole population with something to prove, climbing, comparing, elbowing for status, and showing off whatever they had. Hold that picture, because the church Paul planted will catch the very same fever. Who follows the more impressive teacher, who has the flashier spiritual gift, whose wisdom wins the argument. The cracks running through Corinth would run straight through its church, and that is exactly the wound these two letters were written to heal.

📖 This Week's Memory Text
Acts 18:9-10
☩ Open in Bible App
9One night the Lord spoke to Paul in a vision: “Do not be afraid; keep on speaking; do not be silent. 10For I am with you and no one will lay a hand on you, because I have many people in this city.”

Picture Paul near the end of his rope. He has been run out of one city after another, beaten and mocked, and now in Corinth the synagogue has turned on him too. One night, when he is ready to pack up and leave, Jesus shows up in a vision. Do not be afraid. Keep speaking. Do not go silent. And then the promise that changes everything, that He is with Paul and already has many people in this city. The word translated "people" is the Greek laos, which the Bible normally saves for Israel, God's own covenant nation. Here Jesus spends it on a city full of pagans who have not believed yet. He calls them His before they are converted. The people Paul is afraid of turn out to be the very people God is already claiming.

That one promise sets the table for everything we read this week. We watch Paul introduce himself as an apostle called by God's will and not his own ambition (1 Corinthians 1:1, Galatians 1:1). We follow him from the philosophers of Athens (Acts 17:16-34) into the crowded markets of Corinth, see the moral chaos his letter would later confront (1 Corinthians 5:9-11), watch God meet him in the dark with this strange night-time courage (Acts 18:4-10), and feel the tears behind the letters he sent (2 Corinthians 2:4). One frightened missionary, one steadying voice, and a whole city already full of people who belonged to God before they ever knew His name.

🗓️ This Week's Road Map
  • ☩Sunday: Paul, a God-called Apostle of Jesus. Before we look at the church's problems, we look at the man God sent, and learn that his authority came from a call he did not give himself.
  • ☩Monday: From Athens to Corinth. We trace Paul's road from the lecture halls of Athens to the docks of Corinth and find the one thing he refused to leave behind.
  • ☩Tuesday: The City of Corinth. We walk the streets Paul walked, past the temples and the marketplace, to feel the exact pressures the gospel had to survive.
  • ☩Wednesday: "Many People in This City." Opposition rises, Paul wavers, and God answers a discouraged servant with a promise that there are more of His people here than he can see.
  • ☩Thursday: Paul's Letters to the Corinthians. We discover that behind these famous letters is a pastor's aching heart, writing through tears to people he could not stop loving.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought & Discussion. The week lands on one question: how do we stay faithful in a culture that wants to shape us into its own image?
✝️ The Christ Connection

Here is the thread to watch all quarter. Paul will walk into the most status-obsessed city in Greece, a place that worshiped cleverness and success, and he will refuse to dress up his message to fit in. He will plant his whole ministry on one unimpressive sentence, that he decided to know nothing among them except Jesus Christ and Him crucified. A crucified Savior was an insult to Roman power and a joke to Greek wisdom. Paul preached Him anyway, because the cross is not a weaker version of the gospel we upgrade later. It is the gospel. As the week opens, notice that the message which looked too foolish and too feeble for a sophisticated city turned out to be the only thing strong enough to save it. The same Christ is still strong enough for yours.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Before Sunday, name the messiest community you are actually part of, the church that frustrates you, the family that exhausts you, the workplace you have half written off. Instead of cataloging what is wrong with it this week, ask God to show you what He told Paul about Corinth. He has people there you cannot see yet. Write the name of that community somewhere you will see it midweek, and pray over it once a day with that promise in mind.
🙏 Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we start this new quarter honest about the fact that our churches and our own hearts look more like Corinth than we would like to admit. Thank You that You did not wait for that city to clean itself up before You claimed it. As we open these letters, give us Paul's nerve to hold on to the simple message of the cross when we are tempted to trade it for something more impressive. Show us the people You are already drawing toward Yourself in the places we have given up on. And steady us this week the way You steadied him in the dark, with the reminder that You are with us and the work is not ours to finish alone. In Jesus' name, Amen.
