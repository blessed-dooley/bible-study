Having Faith

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 8, Having Faith Part 1 of 7
May 16, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

This week the Bible's witness on faith assembles in front of us: a Roman centurion who would not let Jesus walk into his house, a Canaanite mother who would not be turned away even when called a dog, a father with tears in his eyes saying "I believe; help my unbelief," and a chapter of names so long the writer of Hebrews finally just runs out of room.

  • ☩The week trades the question "how do I have more faith?" for the older question: what is faith, actually, and where does Jesus find it when He looks at us?
  • ☩Sunday's Pharisees demand a sign and are refused; Monday's centurion will not let Jesus walk one extra step and is praised. Faith on the Bible's terms is not a feeling we generate but a posture toward Someone we are willing to take at His word.
  • ☩The Memory Text hands us two ancient words. Faith is the "reality" of what we hope for (the title deed before we can step onto the land) and the "evidence" of what we cannot see (the proof that holds up in court). Faith is a kind of sight.
  • ☩The week climbs to its climax on Thursday with one phrase: "the faith of Jesus." The same faith we are asked to walk in is first the faith He walked in for us, and Hebrews calls Him "the founder and perfecter" of it.
🔁 Connection to Last Week

Last week the verb was shaphak, and the room kept changing: a juniper in the wilderness, a doorpost at Shiloh, a Galilean hillside, an upper room in Babylon, a rented house in Rome. Five teachers, one inclined Ear, one practice. Pour out your heart. But under every pour-out sat a conviction. The Father was actually there. The Ear was actually inclined. Whatever spilled was actually being received. This week asks the next question. What do you call that conviction? What is the steady weight that lets a person address the unseen God before any visible proof arrives? You call it faith. Faith is not the sequel to last week's prayer. Faith is what made last week's prayer possible.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Hallway of Witnesses

Hebrews 11 is built like an old gallery hall: name on a wall, name on a wall, paragraph after paragraph of people the writer can only briefly sketch because the room is too full for full portraits. Abel speaking still from a grave thousands of years cold. Enoch walking until the path simply ran out of earth. Noah hammering for a hundred years on a coastline that had never seen rain. Abraham camping in tents in a country he had been promised but never owned. Sarah laughing past hope. Moses choosing the wrong people on purpose. Rahab tying a red cord in a window. Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, the prophets, women receiving their dead by resurrection, men sawn in two. The writer finally throws up his hands at verse 32 and admits that time would fail him to tell of them all. What every one of these had in common was not their circumstances, their personalities, their gifts, or even their visible results. Most of them died without seeing what they had been promised. What they shared was the operating system the chapter names in verse 1. They moved through this world by the conviction of unseen reality.

The first hearers of the letter to the Hebrews were a community of Jewish believers under quiet pressure to take the cross back down off the wall and slip safely into synagogue Judaism. The Greek word for faith on every page of their letter is pistis, and in their world that word did real work. In the marketplace it meant good faith on a contract. In the courtroom it meant the testimony you trusted. In personal relationships it meant the loyalty that does not break when the weather turns. The writer chose pistis on purpose. Faith in the Bible's sense is not a mood that runs hot and cold with the news cycle; it is the steady, evidence-based loyalty of a person to the Person of God. The witnesses on the chapter 11 wall are not hung up as moral heroes for us to imitate by force of will. They are hung up as people who took the LORD at His word and kept walking, sometimes for decades, sometimes to the grave, before the next page of the story was visible. The week ahead is an invitation to add our names quietly to the same wall.

📖 This Week's Memory Text
Hebrews 11:1
☩ Open in Bible App
Now faith is the assurance of what we hope for and the certainty of what we do not see.

The writer of Hebrews opens chapter eleven with a definition, which is unusual in his style. He does not narrate, he does not exhort, he simply says: this is what faith does. Faith shows the reality of what we hope for. Faith is the evidence of things we cannot see. The two Greek words behind those English ones carry most of the weight. Hupostasis is a title deed, the legal document you hold in your hand while the property is still in escrow. The deed is the reality of the land before you can put your feet on it. Elenchos is the kind of proof that ends an argument, the testimony in court that settles the matter. Faith, in the Bible's grammar, is not the absence of evidence. Faith is a different kind of evidence, the kind that holds up before the eye can verify it.

Notice what this does for people who have wondered whether their faith was strong enough. The first verse of the Bible's chapter on faith does not put the weight on the strength of our grip. It puts the weight on the certainty of what we are gripping. The deed is real even when our hands are shaking. The witnesses scattered through this week (the Pharisees in Mark 8:11-12 who demanded a sign and were refused, the centurion and the Canaanite mother in Luke 7:1-10 and Matthew 15:21-28 who took Jesus at His word at first hearing, and the long roll call of Hebrews 11 from Abel forward) all built their lives on something the eye could not yet verify. They were not less rational than the people around them. They were rational about a wider reality.

Where does this kind of faith come from? Not from us. Romans 12:3 says God has dealt to each one a measure of faith, the way a parent places a starter coin in a child's open palm. Ephesians 2:8-9 goes further: "For you are saved by grace through faith, and this is not from yourselves; it is God's gift. It is not from works, so that no one can boast." The faith that saves is itself a gift, and the gift forecloses any room for taking credit. We do not pump it up by emotion or force it by willpower. We receive it by grace and exercise what we have been given. The week's faculty puts that to us five days in a row. We are not asked to manufacture faith out of thin air. We are asked to use what God has already placed in our hands.

The road map ends on Thursday with one last verse from this week's pool. Revelation 14:12 describes the end-time people of God as those who "keep the commands of God and the faith of Jesus." That is the climax. The faith Jesus walked in (in the wilderness, in Gethsemane, on the cross) is offered to His followers, not as something we manufacture but as something we inherit from Him. He is the One who started the whole movement and is also bringing it to completion. By the end of the week, the Memory Text will read less like a textbook definition and more like a family deed signed in His blood.

✝️ The Christ Connection

By Thursday this week we will be standing in Gethsemane, reading the prayer of a Man whose faith was being tested at the very edge of what a human body can hold. "My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me; yet not as I will, but as You will." That sentence is the deepest expression of faith in the Bible, not because Jesus did not feel the weight, but because He felt every gram of it and still placed Himself in the Father's hand. Pistis in the New Testament finally has a face. The faith we are being invited into this week is not first our reach toward God; it is the steady trust of the Son toward the Father, which He shared with us at the cross. Hebrews calls Him the founder and perfecter of our faith, the One who started the whole movement and is also bringing it to completion. The week is going to keep zooming in. By Friday it will be clear that what makes our small faith dependable is not the strength of our reach but the strength of His grip on us.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Take an index card or a sticky note and write Hebrews 11:1 on it in your own handwriting (the BSB passage we just read). Put the card somewhere your eyes will hit it three or four times before sundown tomorrow: the bathroom mirror, the dashboard, the inside of your wallet, the spot on the kitchen counter where the coffee maker lives. Each time you see it, read it aloud once. Then ask one short question: "Where am I waiting on a sign today when God is asking me for trust?" Carry the card into the rest of the week. This is the verse the next six days are built on.
🙏 Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we come into this week with hearts that have asked, more times than we can count, for one more sign, one more proof, one more visible reassurance before we move. Forgive us where we have measured You by what we can see, instead of by what You have already said. Teach us this week that faith is not the absence of evidence but a different kind of evidence, and that the deed is real even when our hands are shaking. Show us the Roman centurion who took You at Your word, and the foreign mother who would not be turned away, and the father with tears in his eyes asking You to help his unbelief. Show us Hebrews 11 the way the early church first heard it, as a hallway of family pictures with one empty space waiting for our names. And by Thursday, when we read the words "the faith of Jesus," carry us into the prayer in Gethsemane where the Son trusted the Father at the highest cost any of us will ever know. We do not have great faith, Father. We have a great Object of faith. Help us this week to put our small faith in Him. In Jesus' name, Amen.
