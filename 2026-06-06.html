Setbacks

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 11, Setbacks Part 1 of 7
June 6, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

A girl runs home through a sudden downpour and keeps lifting her face to the sky, because she has decided the storm is God taking her picture. This week is about what we do with our faces when life turns on us. Scripture quietly refuses the old reflex that every setback is a verdict from heaven. Instead it shows storms, sickness, ruin, and collapsed hope becoming the soil where pressure grows perseverance, perseverance grows character, and character grows a hope that does not disappoint.

🔁 Connection to Last Week

Last week ended at the door of a wedding hall, with the robe of His righteousness held out to anyone willing to wear it, given freely, no earning required. But a robe is put on at a door in good weather. This week walks the same person out into the storm. The relationship is real and the forgiveness is settled, so now comes the harder question every long relationship eventually faces. What does it do under pressure? Setbacks are where we find out whether the faith we put on at the door is something we are actually wearing.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Lake That Turns Without Warning

The Sea of Galilee sits in a bowl almost seven hundred feet below sea level, ringed by hills and cut by deep ravines. Cold air slides down off Mount Hermon and the Golan heights, drops through those ravines, and slams into the warm, still air sitting over the water. The collision can turn a glassy evening into a wall of waves in minutes. The men who fished it for a living, Peter and James and John among them, did not fear the lake out of superstition. They feared it because they had watched it do this, and they knew a boat could be swamped before anyone reached shore. The storms this week are not metaphors first. They were weather.

But the ancient world rarely let weather stay weather. A sudden ruin, a wasting illness, a family wiped out in a single season, all of it was read as a message, and the message was almost always the same. Heaven is displeased, and someone is being paid back. The sea itself stood in the old imagination for chaos and threat, the deep that swallowed people whole. Job's friends will say it plainly, that this much suffering proves this much guilt. The whole week leans hard against that reflex. The God these passages reveal is not standing on the shore keeping score. He is asleep in the boat, reaching for a bleeding woman in a crowd, and walking an unrecognized road beside two people whose hope He has let die.

📖 This Week's Memory Text
Romans 5:3-5
☩ Open in Bible App
3Not only that, but we also rejoice in our sufferings, because we know that suffering produces perseverance; 4perseverance, character; and character, hope. 5And hope does not disappoint us, because God has poured out His love into our hearts through the Holy Spirit, whom He has given us.

Paul does not say we glory in spite of tribulations. He says we glory in them, and the word he uses for tribulation is a Greek term that means pressure, the weight of something heavy pressing down until it crushes. Then he traces what that pressure can produce: endurance, then proven character, then hope. This is not a promise that suffering is good. It is a claim that God can run a setback through a chain that comes out the other end as a hope that "does not disappoint." The reason it holds is the last line. Hope is safe not because our circumstances improve but because God's love has already been poured into us by His Spirit, and the verb stays poured. It does not drain back out when the weather turns.

The week walks that chain through real people. Frightened disciples wake a sleeping Jesus in a swamping boat (Mark 4:35-41). A woman who has bled for twelve years pushes through a crowd to reach Him (Mark 5:21-34). Job, stripped of everything, still insists "I know that my Redeemer lives" (Job 19:23-27) even while he cannot find God in any direction he looks (Job 23:8-12). Two travelers trudge toward Emmaus with their hope buried in a tomb (Luke 24:13-27). And Paul lands it: present sufferings are not worth comparing to the glory coming (Romans 8:18), because God is working all of it together for good (Romans 8:28).

✝️ The Christ Connection

Watch for one Face in every storm this week. The God Job ached to corner and question, the One he was sure would vindicate him if he could only find the courtroom, turns out to be the Man asleep in the back of a sinking boat, so unafraid of the storm that He can rest in it. The same Christ who could silence the wind with a word chose instead to let a desperate woman drain healing out of Him in a crowd, and to let two heartbroken travelers walk for miles beside Him without knowing who He was. He does not always still the weather. Sometimes He sleeps through it, or hides inside it, or lets the hope die for three days before He shows His hands. The question this week is not whether He cares. It is whether we will keep our faces turned toward Him long enough to see who has been in the boat the whole time.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Name the storm you are actually in right now. Not a hypothetical one, the real setback sitting on your chest this week. Before Sunday, write out Romans 5:3-5 by hand on a card and put it somewhere you will see it in the middle of that exact struggle, the bathroom mirror, the truck dashboard, the edge of the laptop. Let the week test whether you can read it there and still lift your face.
🙏 Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we come in carrying setbacks we did not choose and did not see coming. Some of us are in the boat with water at our ankles, sure You are asleep. Some of us have been bleeding quietly for years. This week, teach us to stop reading every hard thing as a verdict against us. Show us that You are not on the shore keeping score but in the storm with us, closer than the wind. Pour Your love into us so deeply this week that our hope stops depending on the weather. In Jesus' name, Amen.

"When the last real setback hit you, what was the first thing it made you believe about God? And what would change this week if that belief turned out to be the storm talking, not the truth?"
