Everlasting Love

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 1, Reality Check Part 4 of 7
March 31, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌TL;DR
Before you ever thought about God, He was already thinking about you. Before you ever ran from Him, He was already walking toward you.
  • ☩God designed human beings for intimate relationship with Himself from the very first breath
  • ☩When Adam and Eve hid after their sin, God did not wait for them to come back; He went looking for them
  • ☩Jeremiah 31:3 reveals that God's love is not a response to our behavior; it is everlasting, predating our existence
  • ☩The overcoming promised in Revelation 3:21 is not something we achieve alone; it is possible because Jesus overcame first
🔁Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday we saw Jesus standing at the door and knocking, offering a meal and a relationship to the very church He had just rebuked. Today the study zooms out from Laodicea and asks a bigger question: has God always been like this? The answer takes us all the way back to the Garden of Eden and forward through Jeremiah to show that the pursuing, seeking, never-giving-up love of God is not a New Testament innovation. It is the oldest story there is.

🗺️Historical & Cultural Context
The God Who Walks

In the ancient Near East, the gods kept their distance. Mesopotamian deities were remote, unpredictable, and largely indifferent to individual humans. Temples existed not because the gods wanted to be near people, but because humans needed to appease them. The relationship was transactional: you offered sacrifices so the gods would not destroy your crops or send plagues. Intimacy was not part of the arrangement.

Against that backdrop, Genesis 2-3 is revolutionary. The God of Israel does not sit on a distant throne and wait for tribute. He forms the man with His own hands from the dust. He breathes into his nostrils. He plants a garden and places the man in it. And then, in one of the most stunning verses in the entire Old Testament, He walks in the garden "in the breeze of the day" (Genesis 3:8), implying this was a regular habit, a daily appointment. The God of the Bible is not a deity you visit at a temple once a year. He is a God who shows up in the cool of the day, looking for conversation.

📖Into the Word

Today's study traces God's desire for relationship across the sweep of Scripture, from Creation through the prophets to the promise Jesus gives the overcomer. The anchor passage is Jeremiah 31:3-4, which declares God's love to be everlasting. The supporting texts in Genesis 2:7, Genesis 3:8-10, Revelation 3:21, and Exodus 34:29 show how God has always initiated, always pursued, and always offered restoration.

Genesis 2:7
☩ Open in Bible App
Then the LORD God formed man from the dust of the ground and breathed the breath of life into his nostrils, and the man became a living being.

The Hebrew word for "formed" here is yatsar (יָצַר), the same word used for a potter shaping clay with his hands. This is not distant, delegated creation. This is tactile, personal, intimate. God did not speak humanity into existence the way He spoke light or land into being. He got His hands in the dirt. And the word for "breathed" (naphach, נָפַח) implies mouth-to-mouth closeness. From the very first moment of human existence, God was as close as breath. That is the baseline. That is what relationship with God was designed to look like before sin entered the picture.

Genesis 3:8-10
☩ Open in Bible App
8Then the man and his wife heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the breeze of the day, and they hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God among the trees of the garden. 9But the LORD God called out to the man, “Where are you?” 10“I heard Your voice in the garden,” he replied, “and I was afraid because I was naked; so I hid myself.”

This is the first broken relationship in history. Adam and Eve had sinned, and their instinct was to hide. But notice who moves first. God does not wait for them to come find Him. He walks toward them. He calls out. And His question, "Where are you?" is not the question of someone who does not know the answer. God knew exactly where they were. The question was for their sake, an invitation to stop hiding and face the truth. Sin always makes us want to run and conceal ourselves. God's response, every single time, is to come looking. That pattern never changes across the rest of Scripture.

Jeremiah 31:3-4
☩ Open in Bible App
3The LORD appeared to us in the past, saying: “I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have drawn you with loving devotion. 4Again I will build you, and you will be rebuilt, O Virgin Israel. Again you will take up your tambourines and go out in joyful dancing.

This is the anchor for today. The Hebrew word for "everlasting" is olam (עוֹלָם), meaning a love that stretches beyond the horizons of time in both directions, without beginning and without end. And the phrase "drawn" translates mashak (מָשַׁךְ), which literally means "to draw, to pull." God does not simply love from a distance; He draws us to Himself. He is an active pursuer. And notice what follows the declaration of love: rebuilding. "I will build you, and you will be rebuilt." God's love is not sentimental. It is constructive. It sees the ruin and starts planning the restoration. No matter what your relationship with God is like today, God is seeking you out, waiting to draw you closer to Him. He wants to build, or rebuild, your relationship with Him. If it is not happening, the fault is on your end, not His.

Revelation 3:21
☩ Open in Bible App
To the one who overcomes, I will grant the right to sit with Me on My throne, just as I overcame and sat down with My Father on His throne.

After the devastating diagnosis of Laodicea, after the rebuke and the knock at the door, Jesus ends His letter with the most extraordinary promise in all seven church letters. He does not just offer forgiveness. He offers His throne. The word "overcomes" (Greek: nikao, νικάω) means to conquer, to prevail. But the key phrase is "just as I overcame." Jesus overcame the greatest battle ever fought, the battle against sin and death at the cross. And what is so amazing is that He identifies with us. He understands our apathetic, lukewarm condition. He died to save us from it, overcame sin and its penalty, and now promises to help us do the same. We do not overcome alone. We overcome because He already did.

🎯Street Level
The Parent at 2 AM

You know the phone call. It is two in the morning. Your teenager is at a party they were not supposed to be at, and something went wrong. They are scared, possibly in trouble, definitely not where they said they would be. And they call you. Not because they deserve your help. Not because they have earned it. Because they know, somewhere underneath the shame and the fear, that you will come get them anyway.

And you do. You get out of bed, you put on your shoes, and you drive. Not because what they did was okay. Not because there will not be a conversation tomorrow. But because your love for them is not conditional on their obedience. It existed before they were born, and it will be there after every mistake they make. That is Jeremiah 31:3 in a minivan at 2 AM. God's love is not a reward for good behavior. It is the reason He shows up when everything has fallen apart.

🌍Today's World, Ancient Truth
Why Your Brain Was Built for Attachment

A 2025 study published in Frontiers in Neurology by researchers at Northwestern University examined the relationship between attachment styles and connection to God, using brain imaging of combat veterans with traumatic brain injuries. The findings were striking: individuals with secure attachment styles showed a distinct neural pattern when processing their connection to God, specifically in the prefrontal cortex, the brain region responsible for relational processing and emotional regulation. People with dismissive attachment styles, those who had learned early in life to avoid depending on others, were significantly less likely to experience a meaningful connection to God.

The researchers concluded that our capacity to connect with God is not purely intellectual or doctrinal; it is shaped by the same relational wiring that governs how we attach to other people. The way you learned to relate to your earliest caregivers influences how you experience the God who formed you from dust and breathed into your nostrils.

That finding illuminates today's passage in both directions. It explains why some people read Jeremiah 31:3 and feel nothing: their relational wiring has been damaged by human caregivers who were absent, unpredictable, or harsh. And it reveals why God's persistent, seeking love matters so much. He is not just declaring His love. He is actively rewiring the attachment, walking into the garden, calling out "Where are you?" and refusing to leave until the relationship is restored. He meets us where our wiring is broken and begins to rebuild.

✝️The Christ Connection

The trajectory from Genesis 2 to Revelation 3 is the trajectory of the gospel itself. God formed us for closeness. We hid. He came looking. We kept running. He kept pursuing. And finally, in the ultimate act of seeking, God put on human skin and walked into the garden of this broken world in person. Jesus is God's "Where are you?" made flesh. He came not to a garden but to a manger, not in the cool of the evening but into the heat of human suffering. And at the cross, He absorbed the full weight of every reason we have ever had to hide. The God who breathed life into Adam breathed His last on a Roman cross so that the relationship we shattered could be rebuilt, exactly as Jeremiah 31:4 promised.

✅Personal Application Challenge
Identify one area of your life where you are hiding from God. Not an area where you are openly struggling, but a corner you have quietly closed off, hoping He will not look there. Today, in prayer, open that door. You do not have to have it figured out. Just say, "I have been hiding here." Let Him walk in.
🙏Closing Prayer

Father, You have been seeking us since the very first day. Before we knew Your name, You knew ours. Before we ran, You were already walking toward us. Thank You for a love that does not depend on our performance, a love that is older than our oldest sin. Today we heard You calling, "Where are you?" and we do not want to hide anymore. We are here, Lord. Broken, unfinished, but here. Rebuild us. Draw us to Yourself with that everlasting love that Jeremiah described, the love that never lets go, never gives up, never runs out. We are done running. In Jesus' name, Amen.
