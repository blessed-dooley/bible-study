Christlike Servanthood

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 3, Unity in Christ Part 5 of 7
July 15, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

On the night He was betrayed, Jesus' disciples were arguing about which of them was greatest, and He answered by kneeling to wash their feet. That is the picture Paul drops on a status-obsessed church. Stop ranking your leaders like celebrities. A real leader is a servant and a steward, handed Someone else's treasure to pass on faithfully and owning none of it. And the model is Christ, who held the highest place there is and emptied Himself all the way down to a cross. You cannot claw for rank while wearing the mind of a Savior who climbed down for you.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday ended at the cross, with Paul insisting that God's deepest wisdom is a crucified Man, and that growing up means letting His mind reshape how we treat people. Today he shows us exactly what that mind looks like when it actually runs a life. It looks like a servant. The Corinthians kept ranking their leaders like celebrities and arguing over whose was greatest, and Paul hands them a completely different job description. A true leader is not a star to rally behind. He is a servant and a steward, someone trusted with Someone else's treasure, and the pattern for all of it is Jesus, who had every reason to grasp for status and instead let all of it go.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Manager of the Estate Usually Owned Nothing

In a wealthy Roman household, the most capable person on the property was often the estate manager, and he was frequently a slave. He held the keys, kept the accounts, directed the staff, and was trusted with the master's entire fortune. Real authority ran through his hands every single day. And yet he owned none of it. The house was not his, the money was not his, the land was not his. Everything he managed belonged to someone else, and his whole standing hung on one question, whether he was faithful to his master. A brilliant steward who started skimming for himself was not clever. He was a thief.

Paul reaches for exactly that figure to reset how Corinth sees its leaders. An apostle is not a celebrity to be ranked and cheered. He is the household manager, handed the master's treasure, the gospel itself, to distribute faithfully rather than to build a personal following on. The one thing asked of a steward is not brilliance or charisma or a bigger crowd than the steward down the road. It is faithfulness to the owner. And a steward who starts collecting fans for himself is quietly robbing the Master. In a city that measured leaders by applause, Paul measures them by loyalty to Someone else, and reminds everyone that the treasure was never theirs to fight over in the first place.

📖 Into the Word

Two pictures sit at the heart of today, and together they take the whole competition apart. First Paul tells the Corinthians how to think about their leaders, not as headliners but as servants and stewards. Then he shows them where a posture like that even comes from, the mind of Christ Himself, who held the highest status in the universe and spent all of it going down. Put the two side by side and the factions look absurd. Just before this Paul had branded the Corinthians' quarreling a mark of spiritual infancy, the worldly behavior of people still lining up behind rival names and boasting that they belonged to Paul or to Apollos (1 Corinthians 3:1-4). You cannot rank servants the way Corinth was ranking them, and you cannot scramble for status while following a Master who emptied Himself. Watch Paul move from the job description of a leader to the heart of the Leader who defines the whole thing.

1 Corinthians 4:1-2
☩ Open in Bible App
1So then, men ought to regard us as servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God. 2Now it is required of stewards that they be found faithful.

Paul hands the church a whole new way to see the people they were so busy ranking. Not celebrities. Servants and managers. The word for manager is oikonomos, the household steward, the trusted servant who ran a wealthy master's entire estate, and the word behind our "economy." He carried the keys and the accounts and real authority, and still he owned none of it. Everything he managed belonged to someone else. That, Paul says, is the job. Leaders are stewards handed the mysteries of God to pass along faithfully, not treasure to hoard and not a fan base to build. And look at the single thing actually required of a steward. Not brilliance, not charisma, not a bigger crowd than the steward next door. Faithfulness to the Master. Rank the servants against each other and you have missed the entire point. There is only one Owner, and none of it was ever yours to fight over.

So where does a servant heart like that even come from? Paul has already dropped the astonishing claim that we have the mind of Christ (1 Corinthians 2:16). Now he shows that mind in full motion, in maybe the most breathtaking description of downward movement ever written.

Philippians 2:5-8
☩ Open in Bible App
5Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus: 6Who, existing in the form of God, did not consider equality with God something to be grasped, 7but emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in human likeness. 8And being found in appearance as a man, He humbled Himself and became obedient to death—even death on a cross.

Here is the mind that heals a divided church. Jesus began with the highest status there is, equal with God, and He refused to clutch it. He let it go. He emptied Himself, took the nature of a servant, and kept descending, all the way down to a criminal's cross. Trace it as a straight line and it is dizzying. From the throne of heaven, to a servant's place, to a Roman execution, there was no bottom Jesus was unwilling to reach. And Paul aims this directly at the quarreling. He tells the Philippians, who had their own divisions simmering, to count others more important than themselves and to watch out for one another's interests (Philippians 2:1-4). That is what the mind of Christ does inside a real church. It quits asking who is greatest and starts asking whom it can serve. You cannot climb over other believers while wearing the mind of a Savior who climbed down for them.

🎯 Street Level
The Name That Is Never on the Wall

Behind a famous painting there is almost always a person no one thinks about. The conservator. She is trusted with a canvas worth more than she will earn in ten lifetimes, and she handles it more closely than anyone alive, cleaning it under careful light, mending a hairline crack with a brush a few hairs wide. She wears gloves so her own skin never touches the surface. And when the work is finished, the painting goes back on the wall with the artist's name beside it, never hers. Millions will stand in front of it and feel something, and not one of them will know she existed. That is the whole job. The treasure was never hers. She was only ever meant to keep it and hand it on unharmed.

Paul hands the Corinthians that exact picture of a leader, and it lands like cold water on a church busy ranking its teachers like celebrities. A real leader, he says, is a steward. Someone trusted with a treasure that is not his, the good news itself, and given one assignment. Be faithful with it. Do not hoard it, do not sign your name across it, do not build a personal following on the back of it. And this is the mind of Christ running all the way down. The One who owned everything took the lowest place and worked unseen, so that we could see the glory of Someone else. A leader who starts collecting admirers has quietly reached up and pried his own name onto a masterpiece he was never meant to sign.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Founder Who Gave the Whole Company Away

In September 2022 Yvon Chouinard, the founder of the outdoor company Patagonia, did something almost nobody sitting on three billion dollars does. He gave the entire company away. He did not sell it, did not take it public, did not hand it to his children to keep. He and his family signed all of it over to a trust and a nonprofit built to guard the company's mission, and announced that from then on every dollar of profit not reinvested, around a hundred million a year, would go to fight the climate crisis. "Earth is now our only shareholder," he said. A man who could have cashed out and lived like a king instead handed away the thing he built and kept working to run it for a purpose far bigger than his own name.

That startles us for the same reason Paul's message startled Corinth. Everything in us assumes a builder should cash in, put his name on the tower, and climb. Chouinard walked the other direction on purpose, and the whole world stopped to stare, which is exactly the point. A life poured downward is so rare it makes headlines. Yet Paul insists downward is simply the shape of Christian leadership, running straight from the example of Christ, who owned everything and grasped at none of it. The Corinthians were scrambling upward, fighting over whose teacher outranked whose. The gospel calls a leader the opposite way, toward the low place, measuring greatness not by what you can pile up and keep but by what you can hold loosely and give away.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Sit with the timing of it. On the same night Jesus was walking toward the cross, His disciples were arguing about which of them was the greatest. He answered them not with a lecture but with a towel, kneeling to wash the feet of men too busy competing to wash His. That is the mind Paul is describing. Everything the Corinthians were fighting over, rank, recognition, a place above the others, Jesus already held by right and gave away on purpose. He is God, and He chose the basin and the cross. So the deepest offense in a divided church is not simply that we bicker about status. It is that we bicker about status while the Son of God is kneeling in front of us with a towel in His hands. The cross does not only forgive our pride. It exposes it, and then it offers to trade it for something far better, a heart that finds its glory in going low for people who can never pay it back.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Find one place today where you are quietly competing for rank, at work, in your family, in your church, or even inside your own head, keeping score of who gets the credit or who is ahead. Then pick one concrete act of low, unglamorous service you can do there this week, the kind nobody applauds. Clean the thing no one wants to clean. Hand someone else the credit. Serve a person who cannot return the favor. The mind of Christ is not admired into you. It is practiced into you, one basin at a time.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, You had every right to be served, and You reached for a towel. We confess how far our instincts run from Yours. We angle for credit, we keep score, and we want to be the greatest even in rooms where You are kneeling to wash feet. Forgive our endless scramble for rank. Give us Your mind, the mind that released its status and went low for people who could never repay it. Teach us to measure ourselves by faithfulness to You instead of applause from others, and to find our joy in serving the way You served us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
