Wisdom and Maturity

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 3, Unity in Christ Part 4 of 7
July 14, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

A church that prided itself on being wise gets handed the most deflating word Paul has. Babies. Still on milk, not ready for solid food, and the proof is the bickering, the jealousy, the endless "I follow Paul" and "I follow Apollos." To Corinth that partisanship felt sophisticated. To Paul it was a nursery squabble. Real maturity, he says, is not knowing more facts or admiring smarter teachers. It is being trained by walking with God until you can actually tell what matters. The taking of sides they thought made them look wise was the clearest sign they had never grown up.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday Paul handed us the cure in a single line. Put Christ back in the center and the factions lose their oxygen. But that leaves a painful question hanging. If the answer is that simple, why could the Corinthians not just do it? Today Paul tells them, and it stings. They could not act like a mature church because they were not one yet. They were still spiritual infants, and their fan clubs were the evidence. The very slogans they were so proud of, I follow Paul, I belong to Apollos, were baby talk. Paul loves them too much to flatter them, so he calls them back to the solid food they keep pushing away.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
Milk and Solid Food Were a Teacher's Everyday Shorthand

In the ancient world, teachers reached for the same picture Paul uses here all the time. A beginner got milk, the simple first lessons anyone could swallow. The advanced student, weaned onto solid food, could finally chew on the hard and heavy things. Rabbis used it, philosophers used it, and everybody understood the insult buried inside it. A grown adult still demanding a bottle was a picture of someone who had refused to grow up. And in Corinth, of all places, that stung, because this was a city where being counted wise and mature was a genuine status symbol, something people competed for the way they competed for money and titles.

So Paul's diagnosis lands with surgical precision. The Corinthians were sure their sophistication showed in their taste for impressive teachers and their skill at picking sides. Paul says it shows the opposite. All that clever partisanship is not the mark of wise adults. It is a nursery full of toddlers squabbling over which grown-up they like best. And here is the quiet tragedy underneath it. They wanted to be seen as wise more than they wanted to actually become wise, and the road to real maturity ran straight through the one thing their pride would not allow. Admit you are still on milk.

📖 Into the Word

Paul has been fairly gentle so far. Here he stops cushioning it. He looks at a church that prided itself on wisdom and calls them babies, not as an insult thrown in anger but as a diagnosis given in love, the way a doctor names a condition so it can finally be treated. The factions, the sides, the whole competitive circus, all of it was evidence of one thing. They had never grown up. So Paul reaches for the oldest picture in the book, milk and solid food, to show them how far they still have to travel, and to call them toward the real wisdom they keep trading away for something cheaper.

1 Corinthians 3:1-4
☩ Open in Bible App
1Brothers, I could not address you as spiritual, but as worldly—as infants in Christ. 2I gave you milk, not solid food, for you were not yet ready for solid food. In fact, you are still not ready, 3for you are still worldly. For since there is jealousy and dissension among you, are you not worldly? Are you not walking in the way of man? 4For when one of you says, “I follow Paul,” and another, “I follow Apollos,” are you not mere men?

Paul picks the most deflating word he can find. He calls a church obsessed with looking wise a room full of nepioi, the Greek word for babies, infants who can only take milk because they cannot yet chew. Then he points to the evidence sitting right in front of them. The jealousy. The strife. The chant of "I follow Paul" and "I follow Apollos." To the Corinthians all that partisanship felt like discernment, a sign they had refined taste in teachers. To Paul it was a nursery squabble. Grown-up faith does not fracture into cliques over personalities, and only the immature mistake taking sides for depth. And what were they fighting to own? A church that was never theirs to divide, God's own field and building and temple, the place where we all belong to God through Christ (1 Corinthians 3:9, 1 Corinthians 3:11, 1 Corinthians 3:16-17).

Hebrews 5:13-14
☩ Open in Bible App
13For everyone who lives on milk is still an infant, inexperienced in the message of righteousness. 14But solid food is for the mature, who by constant use have trained their senses to distinguish good from evil.

Paul is not the only one who used this picture. The book of Hebrews scolds a different set of believers the very same way, telling people who ought to be teachers by now that they still need someone to walk them through the ABCs of the faith (Hebrews 5:12). Here is the sharp part. Staying on milk is not a harmless holding pattern. It leaves a person inexperienced with the deeper things of God, unable to handle real weight, because those things belong to the mature, the ones whose spiritual senses have been trained by practice to tell good from evil. So maturity is not about how many years you have been a Christian or how much Bible trivia you can recite. It is about whether your walk with God has actually trained you to live wisely. You can carry a Bible for fifty years and still be crying for a bottle.

1 Corinthians 2:6-7
☩ Open in Bible App
6Among the mature, however, we speak a message of wisdom—but not the wisdom of this age or of the rulers of this age, who are coming to nothing. 7No, we speak of the mysterious and hidden wisdom of God, which He destined for our glory before time began.

So what is the solid food they keep refusing? Real wisdom, but not the kind Corinth was chasing. Paul says there is a wisdom he speaks among the mature, and it is nothing like the slick wisdom of this age that is already on its way out. It is God's own hidden wisdom, a plan He laid before time began, and it reaches us not through raw brainpower but through His Spirit, who alone lets us grasp the things of God (1 Corinthians 2:13-14). And where is that wisdom found? Not in a lecture hall. In the cross (1 Corinthians 2:1-4), the very thing the clever called foolish. This is the whole point Paul is making before he circles back to their unity (1 Corinthians 3:1-17). The Corinthians desperately wanted to be wise. The door to real wisdom stood open the entire time, and it was shaped like a cross they kept walking past on their way to argue about their favorite teachers.

🎯 Street Level
What the Old Farmer Feels Coming

Put two people at the edge of the same field. One just finished an agriculture degree and can recite the science of soil chemistry and rainfall. The other never went to college but has worked this ground for forty years. A gray smudge shows up on the horizon. The graduate pulls out a phone to check the forecast. The old farmer has already started moving, because they felt the shift in the wind and smelled the rain long before the app will admit it is coming. Bend down and crumble the dirt between your fingers, and the veteran knows by the way it holds together whether the ground is ready. The book learning is real. It is just not the same thing as knowing.

That is the difference Paul is pressing on a church that was very proud of how wise it was. The Corinthians could debate theology and rank their favorite teachers with real skill, and they mistook that for maturity. Paul calls them babies anyway. Real growth, he says, is not how many facts you have stacked up. It is whether your senses have been trained, by actually walking with God through a lot of ordinary years, to tell what matters from what does not. You can memorize the whole field guide and still panic at the first yellow leaf. You can sit in a pew your entire life and still never learn to read the ground under your own feet. The solid food was never more information. It is the slow, trained wisdom that finally shows up in how you treat the people around you.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Board a Master Reads in Five Seconds

Show a chess grandmaster a real game position for just five seconds, then take it away, and they can rebuild it on an empty board almost perfectly, some thirty pieces back in their right places. It looks like a superhuman memory. Then researchers tried something clever. They scattered the same number of pieces across the board at random, with no real game behind them, and gave the master another five-second look. Suddenly the grandmaster remembered no more than a rank beginner. The famous experiments by Adriaan de Groot and later Chase and Simon showed the master's gift was never a bigger memory or a higher IQ. It was thousands of hours of play that had trained the eyes to see the board in meaningful patterns. Experts, as the researchers put it, do not search more. They see more.

Paul is pressing that exact distinction on people who were sure their cleverness made them mature. And here is the sharp edge of the chess research. Take the master off a real board and hand them a random one, and all that brilliance simply evaporates, because the gift was never raw brainpower. It was pattern, earned slowly in one specific arena. Spiritual maturity works the same way. It is not imported from being generally smart, well-read, or quick in an argument. It grows up close, over years of walking with God, until a person can read a situation the way a master reads the board. The Corinthians had plenty of intelligence and almost no discernment, and they could not tell the two apart. Growing up in Christ is learning to see what is really in front of you, and that kind of sight comes only one slow way.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Notice where Paul says the deepest wisdom of God is actually kept. Not in a library, not in a philosophy, not in the teacher with the best arguments. In a crucified Man. The smartest people in Corinth looked at Jesus dying on a cross and saw the dumbest story ever told, and Paul says that is exactly where the wisdom of God was hiding all along. This is the solid food a divided church could not stomach, that the way up is down, that greatness looks like a Savior washing feet and bleeding for enemies. You cannot grow up in that wisdom while you are busy competing for rank, because the cross is the death of rank. Real maturity is not measured by how much you know about Christ. It is measured by how much you have let the mind of the crucified Christ reshape the way you actually treat people. The Corinthians wanted to look wise. Jesus offered to make them wise, and the classroom was Calvary.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Here is an honest gut-check for today. Think about your spiritual "diet" over the last month. Are you actually growing, or just collecting content, sermons, podcasts, hot takes, while the way you treat people has not changed at all? Pick one area where you know God has been asking you to grow up, maybe how you handle disagreement, or a grudge you keep nursing, or a habit you excuse. Take one concrete step in it today. Maturity is not the information you gather. It is the obedience you finally practice.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, we admit that we often mistake being informed for being mature. We can discuss You, quote You, and take sides about You while still acting like toddlers with the people around us. Forgive us for wanting to look wise more than we want to become wise. Wean us off the milk we keep clinging to. Train our senses through the ordinary practice of following You, so that we learn to tell what really matters. And keep drawing us back to the cross, where Your deepest wisdom is hidden, until the mind of Christ reshapes how we live. In Jesus' name, Amen.
