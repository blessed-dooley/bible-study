A Portrait of Love

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 7, A Portrait of Love Part 1 of 7
August 8, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

The most famous chapter Paul ever wrote was mailed into the middle of a church fight. Corinth is still arguing about whose gift ranks highest when thirteen quiet verses arrive and change the subject. Love is patient. Love is kind. Love never ends. This week we slow down and sit inside 1 Corinthians 13, one movement at a time. What love does. What love refuses to do. Whose face appears when the verbs are read slowly. Love is not one more item on the Christian list. It is the portrait of Jesus, painted in verbs, and we are invited to sit for it.

🔁 Connection to Last Week

Last week ended in a dark theater, watching credits scroll. Hundreds of names, most of them never recognized anywhere, every one of them necessary, and love was the power source that made the whole body of gifts worth anything. But we only drove past love on Tuesday. We saw what happens when it is missing, the expensive bronze noise, the gifts that add up to nothing. Now Paul makes us park the car and go inside. The current that every gift runs on gets its own week, line by line, verb by verb.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Word Greeks Almost Never Used

A Corinthian had a word for every kind of love. For desire there was erōs, and the city knew that one well, with the goddess of it enthroned on the mountain above the harbor. For friendship there was philia, the warm loyalty between comrades that Greek writers praised as the crown of a good life. For family there was storgē, the bond of a mother and her child. Three famous words, each one busy, each one bent at least partly toward what the lover gets out of it. And then there was a fourth word, agapē, sitting in the back of the vocabulary. Pale. Rare. Almost nobody used it. It had no famous poems, no temple, no story.

That blank word is the one the first Christians picked up. The other three were already spoken for, already shaped by appetite and affection and kinship. So when they needed a name for what they had met in Jesus, a love that chooses, gives, and keeps no accounts, they reached past the famous words and filled the empty one. By the time Paul writes to Corinth, the once-forgettable word is becoming the church's signature. This week he sits down and writes its definition, and the definition will turn out to have a face. The city that had a word for every love is about to learn the one it was missing.

📖 This Week's Memory Text
1 Corinthians 13:13
☩ Open in Bible App
And now these three remain: faith, hope, and love; but the greatest of these is love.

Paul ends his greatest chapter by imagining the end of everything else. Prophecies fulfilled. Tongues quiet. Knowledge outgrown. When the dust of history settles, he says, three things are still standing, and then he ranks them. The greatest is love. The word is agapē, Greek for the love that commits itself and gives, whether or not it is ever paid back. Paul's letters use that word family more than 135 times, nearly half of all its appearances in the entire New Testament, and nowhere does he pour more into it than here. Faith and hope are how we hold on to God. Love is what God is. That is why it outranks even its two immortal companions.

The verse also hands us this week's map. We will live inside the whole chapter (1 Corinthians 13), and we will need it, because Jesus warned that in the last days the love of many would grow cold (Matthew 24:12). We will watch love lead the Spirit's harvest in a believer's life (Galatians 5:22, 23), overflow with the grace that found Paul (1 Timothy 1:14), and finally trace back to its source, the God who is love (1 John 4:8). Scripture is crowded with great love passages. Nothing separates us from God's love (Romans 8:35-39). Eye has not seen what God has prepared for those who love Him (1 Corinthians 2:9). The one who loves God is known by Him (1 Corinthians 8:3). The Son of God loved me and gave Himself for me (Galatians 2:20). The Father delivered us into the kingdom of the Son He loves (Colossians 1:13). May the Lord cause you to increase and overflow with love (1 Thessalonians 3:12). And still, nothing compares to the thirteen verses ahead of us. This is the summit of it all.

🗓️ This Week's Road Map
  • ☩Sunday: The Essentialness of Love. Without love, even tongues, prophecy, faith, and sacrifice add up to nothing, and Jesus warns that love can grow cold.
  • ☩Monday: What Love Does. Paul paints love in seven working verbs, patience and kindness and shared joy, love measured in actions rather than feelings.
  • ☩Tuesday: What Love Does Not Do. Eight refusals, from envy to record-keeping, that show love by what it will not allow itself.
  • ☩Wednesday: A Portrait of Jesus. Read the verbs slowly and a face emerges, because only One Person has ever lived every line.
  • ☩Thursday: Faith, Hope, and Love. Gifts expire and knowledge fades, but three things outlast the world, and the greatest is love.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought & Discussion. The week lands on love as the life of everything, the only cure for a divided church.
✝️ The Christ Connection

Here is the seed to plant before Sunday. As you read the famous chapter this week, try reading it as a wanted poster. Somewhere there is a person who is endlessly patient, relentlessly kind, never envious, never rude, keeping no file of wrongs, bearing everything, believing the best, hoping past reason, outlasting every hurt. Hold the description up against everyone you know, including the face in the mirror, and watch every candidate fall short somewhere around the second verse. The chapter is not just poetry. It is a description of Someone, and by midweek Scripture will say His name out loud. Watch for Him early. He is behind the patience on Monday, underneath every refusal on Tuesday, and waiting in full view on Wednesday. The greatest thing ever written about love was always a portrait, and the portrait was always of Him.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Before Sunday, read 1 Corinthians 13 out loud once, slowly, alone if you can. Thirteen verses takes about ninety seconds. As you read, notice the one phrase that stings a little, the one that sounds least like your last seven days. Write that phrase somewhere you will see it every day this week, the bathroom mirror, a sticky note on the dash, the lock screen on your phone. Do not fix anything yet. Just let the phrase ride along with you. That sting is where this week intends to work.
🙏 Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we have heard these words at weddings and seen them framed on walls, and we confess that we have admired them more than we have lived them. This week, take the most famous chapter Paul ever wrote and make it unfamiliar again. Slow us down at the verbs. Show us where our patience runs out, where our kindness gets selective, where the record of wrongs is still in the drawer. And as the portrait comes into focus, let us see not a standard that condemns us but a Savior who loves us exactly this way. Give us eyes to recognize His face in every line, and hearts willing to be repainted. We do not want to finish this week having studied love. We want to finish it loving. In Jesus' name, Amen.

And what would change by Friday if that relationship got the first fruits of this chapter instead of the leftovers?"
