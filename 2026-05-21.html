The Faith of Jesus

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 8, Having Faith Part 6 of 7
May 21, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

A man is kneeling in an olive grove across a stream from Jerusalem on a Thursday night in early spring, with three of His best friends asleep behind Him and an executioner's torchlight starting to appear at the far end of the path, and He prays the deepest sentence of faith in the Bible: "Not as I will, but as You will."

  • ☩Revelation 14:12 says God's end-time people are marked by two things, the commandments of God and "the faith of Jesus." The phrase reads both ways in the Greek and is meant to read both ways. The faith we have in Him and the faith He has in His Father are both at work in us.
  • ☩The deepest demonstration of faith in human history is not Abraham at the altar or Moses on the mountain. It is the Son in the Garden, asking for the cup to pass and putting Himself in the Father's hand when the answer was no.
  • ☩Hebrews calls Jesus "the founder and perfecter of our faith." He went into the unfelt night first and came back out so that none of us would ever have to walk by feeling alone. The faith He exercised is now the faith He gives.
  • ☩The verse the week was always heading toward is the prayer Hebrews 11:6 hands us as our own: "Lord, I believe You exist, and that You reward those who seek You. I come to You now."
🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday Hebrews 11 walked us down a hallway of family portraits and ended with a strange sentence in verse 40. None of those witnesses were made complete apart from us, the writer said, because God had something better in mind that the chapter could not finish on its own. Today the chapter's Center steps into view. The cloud was waiting for One whose faith would make every other faith on the wall make sense. He walked into a garden the same week the disciples were arguing about which one of them was the greatest, and He prayed a prayer that no one in the cloud had ever been asked to pray. He is not one more name on the chapter 11 wall. He is the One the wall was always pointing at.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Olive Press Across the Brook

The Kidron Valley runs along the eastern wall of Jerusalem. On the far side of the brook, on the lower slope of the Mount of Olives, is a small olive grove with a press. The Aramaic name of the spot was Gethsemane, which means "oil press," the heavy grindstone that crushed olives into the oil used for cooking, lamps, and anointing. The press worked by sheer weight: a stone rolled over fruit until the inside of the fruit came out as oil. The name itself, on the night Jesus walked into the grove, is one of the saddest puns in the gospels. He had come to the place where the pressure would do its work on Him.

The olive trees in that grove today are some of the oldest living trees in the world. Radiocarbon work on a few of the ancient specimens in the Garden has dated portions of their root systems to within centuries of the first-century period, and several trees there are confirmed to be more than nine hundred years old; the surviving stumps are descended directly from the trees Jesus would have walked between. The grove was a private retreat used by Jesus and the disciples often enough that Judas knew exactly where to find Him. The historical setting matters because the prayer in this garden was not theatrical. It was tucked into a familiar corner of the city, on the same dirt the disciples had eaten lunch on, and the Voice in agony was the same Voice they had heard teaching the children that very afternoon. It happened in a room they would recognize. That is part of why the gospel writers slowed down so much when they remembered it. The most cosmic prayer in human history was prayed in the most ordinary garden any of them knew.

📖 Into the Word

Three verses today, and they are arranged in the shape the whole Bible is arranged in. End-time identity, garden agony, slain Lamb. We start with the mark of God's last-days people in Revelation 14, then we walk three days earlier in the timeline to the prayer that made that mark possible, then we close in heaven with the song the angels sing about why the Lamb is worthy to open the scroll. The faith of Jesus is the thread.

Revelation 14:12
☩ Open in Bible App
Here is a call for the perseverance of the saints, who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus.

The Greek phrase at the end of this verse is ten pistin Iesou, and it is one of the most fertile two-word combinations in the New Testament. The grammar is what scholars call a genitive of relationship, and it can be read two ways at once: "faith in Jesus" (the trust we place in Him) and "the faith of Jesus" (the trust that belonged to Him in the days of His flesh). English translations must choose one reading in the line itself, but the original Greek keeps both in view, and the dual reading is not a problem to solve. It is the announcement of how saving faith actually works.

God's end-time people are not a group who have manufactured a heroic level of personal trust. They are a group whose own trust has been joined to the trust the Son of God exercised at the cross. When Revelation describes them, it does so by mentioning two things together. They keep the commandments of God, and they live by the faith of Jesus. The two are not in tension; they are bound at the wrist, the way the law and the gospel were always meant to be. The faith that saves and the obedience that follows it are gifts from the same Person, hand in hand all the way to the end of the world.

Notice what this does for the question that has shadowed us all week. "How much faith do I have?" In Revelation 14, the saints are not measured by the size of their own trust. They are described as those who have the faith of Jesus in their pockets. That faith is the same faith we will see Him exercise three verses from now in a garden on the wrong side of a brook. The weakness of our reach is not the bottleneck. The strength of His grip is what is being credited to our account.

Matthew 26:36-42
☩ Open in Bible App
36Then Jesus went with His disciples to a place called Gethsemane, and He told them, “Sit here while I go over there and pray.” 37He took with Him Peter and the two sons of Zebedee and began to be sorrowful and deeply distressed. 38Then He said to them, “My soul is consumed with sorrow to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch with Me.” 39Going a little farther, He fell facedown and prayed, “My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me. Yet not as I will, but as You will.” 40Then Jesus returned to the disciples and found them sleeping. “Were you not able to keep watch with Me for one hour?” He asked Peter. 41“Watch and pray so that you will not enter into temptation. For the spirit is willing, but the body is weak.” 42A second time He went away and prayed, “My Father, if this cup cannot pass unless I drink it, may Your will be done.”

Matthew gives us the prayer in two stages. The first version is, "Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me. Yet not as I will, but as You will." The second, an hour later when nothing has changed and the friends are asleep again, is "My Father, if this cup cannot pass unless I drink it, may Your will be done." The first prayer leaves an opening. The second prayer closes it. Mark and Luke add the detail that Jesus prayed this prayer three times. By the third round, the answer has settled. The cup will not pass. The Son will drink it. The deepest sentence of faith in human history is not "I am sure everything will work out." It is, "Not as I will, but as You will."

The Greek noun behind "will" is thelema, the same word we have been tracing across two weeks now. Last week it bent every prayer we prayed toward the Father in Hebrews 11:6's posture of drawing near. Tuesday it was the partner-word to "ask, seek, knock." Tonight it sits on the lips of the Son in the garden, choosing the Father's thelema over His own. He is not pretending the agony is not real. Luke says He was in such anguish that His sweat was like drops of blood falling to the ground, and that the Father sent an angel to strengthen Him. The faith on display is not a faith without feeling. It is a faith that holds when the feeling is screaming for an exit.

Look at what He is trusting. He does not have visible evidence in the moment that the resurrection is coming. He has the Word of His Father, the prophetic record that the Servant must suffer, and the steady character of the One He has known from before the foundation of the world. Hebrews 11's witnesses all walked toward unseen rewards. The Son walks toward unseen vindication on a deeper level. None of the cloud was ever asked for a faith of this kind. He is the One the chapter was always making room for, and the prayer in Gethsemane is the moment His faith carried the cloud across.

And there is a small grace tucked into the gospels for us. The friends fell asleep. Three times. The Man who is about to die for them cannot keep them awake for an hour. He does not deliver them a lecture on the inadequacy of their faith. He prays, "Watch and pray so that you will not enter into temptation. For the spirit is willing, but the body is weak." He has been carrying our weakness in His own body all the way to the press. The Gethsemane prayer is the deepest faith ever offered to God, and it is being offered on our behalf by a Man who knows exactly how tired we are.

Revelation 5:9
☩ Open in Bible App
And they sang a new song: “Worthy are You to take the scroll and open its seals, because You were slain, and by Your blood You purchased for God those from every tribe and tongue and people and nation.

This is what the universe sings about the Lamb when no one is watching but heaven. The Greek for "worthy" is axios, the word used of someone whose track record proves the office He holds. The angels do not sing that He is worthy because He is powerful, or because He is divine, or because He is wise. The reason given is more pointed. He is worthy because He was slaughtered. The faith He exercised in the Garden became blood at the cross, and the blood purchased a people from every tribe and language. The Garden prayer was not a moment of private spirituality; it was the hinge that turned the universe.

This means something for the way we hear today's anchor verse all over again. The "faith of Jesus" in Revelation 14:12 is the same faith John watches Jesus exercise in the song of Revelation 5. It is not abstract. It is recorded. It is the prayer in the press, the silent walk to the cross, the cry from the sixth hour to the ninth, and the trust the Father in the resurrection. We do not have to wonder whether the faith we are inheriting has been tested in real conditions. The faith we are asked to walk in this morning was first walked in a garden across a brook, on a hill called the Skull, and through three days of darkness, by the One the cloud was always pointing toward.

🎯 Street Level
Tied In to the Lead

Two climbers are on a granite slab in Yosemite Valley, sixty meters of climbing rope between them. One is a first-time outdoor climber, forty feet up the rock and one hour past the gym wall. The other is the senior partner, lead climber, three hundred ascents on this exact pitch. The novice's hands are sweating. He can feel the rope at his harness, tight. The senior climber, on a small ledge above him, calls down, "I've got you. Move your right foot to the small lip about two inches to your right." The novice cannot quite see the lip from his angle, but the rope cannot lie. The senior climber has both feet planted, his belay device locked, his weight on the anchor. The faith the novice needs to make a move he could not have made on his own is not in his own hands. It is in the rope. And the rope is held by someone who has stood on this exact ledge three hundred times and knows every variable. The novice's job is to lift the foot. The faith that catches him is borrowed, and the borrowing is the move.

Revelation 14:12 is two climbers on a rope. "The faith of Jesus" is the lead climber who already knows every move on this pitch, who has set the anchors, who has tested every hold with His own body, who is standing at the next ledge with the rope locked. The faith we exercise this morning is borrowed from His. The trust He carried into the olive press across the Kidron has the anchor of His Father at the other end of the line, and we are tied in. When the next move is one we cannot make on our own, the rope is already tight. The Person at the top has not blinked. We lift the foot. The grip catching us is His.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Mentor's Trajectory Becomes the Mentee's

In January 2025, Big Brothers Big Sisters of America released a Harvard and U.S. Treasury study based on thirty years of data on the long-term effect of one adult mentor on one child. The data set was nearly a thousand youth originally matched in 1991, then followed for decades. The finding that startled the researchers was not the increase in college enrollment, or the increase in earnings, although both were substantial. It was the income trajectory. Children who had a mentor through adolescence ended up with adult income patterns that more closely tracked the mentor's life trajectory than the family they had been born into. The mentor was not paying the rent. The mentor was not pulling strings. The mentor was simply there, week after week, for years. The mentee's life followed the line of trust. The socioeconomic gap between the mentor's world and the mentee's family of origin closed by roughly two-thirds, not by transfer of resources but by transfer of the trajectory the mentee believed was possible. The researchers had a clean number for what the Bible has been telling us in a different language: the trust we exercise pulls us up the line of the One we have linked our life to.

Hebrews 12 calls Jesus the founder and perfecter of our faith. The path is broken in. The route has been walked all the way through. He went first and finished. The trust we place in Him is not a freestanding athletic event we are required to win on our own. The faith we exercise is being pulled, in real measurable time, toward the trajectory of the One whose name we have taken. The next move was already on His map before it was on ours.

✝️ The Christ Connection

The faith of Jesus is the climax of the week, but it is also the gift the week was building toward. Hebrews 12 calls Him the founder and perfecter of our faith. He started the trust that gets us home, and He is the One bringing it to completion. The cloud in Hebrews 11 walked toward Him before they knew His name. We walk toward Him knowing it. The same Man who knelt in the olive press across the brook is the One whose name we will write on the back of our Memory Text card tomorrow morning, the One whose Spirit sat with us this week through the Pharisees' refusal and the centurion's request and the Canaanite mother's comeback. He has been the answer to every question we have been asking. He is the sign the Pharisees never received. He is the faith Jesus saw in His own followers and named. He is the mustard seed and the soil it was planted in. He is the cloud's missing center. And the prayer Hebrews 11:6 hands us at the end of today is the prayer He has been waiting to hear from us: Lord, I believe You are, and that You reward those who diligently seek You. I come to You now. The week has spent five days walking us toward the door of that simple prayer. Today He opens it.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Pray Hebrews 11:6 in the first person today, slowly, aloud, with your own name spoken at the end. "Lord, without faith it is impossible to please You. I come to You and believe that You are, and that You reward those who diligently seek You. I do so now. Help me, [your name], to walk in the faith of Jesus today." Then pick one specific place in your day where you have been waiting to feel ready before acting in trust, and take the step before the feeling arrives. Let the prayer of Gethsemane sit in your bones: "Not as I will, but as You will."
🙏 Closing Prayer

Father, we come this morning by the path the Son cut for us across the Kidron and up the slope of Olivet, into a garden where He prayed a prayer we could not have prayed. We thank You for the faith He held in the press, three times asking for the cup to pass and three times yielding to Your will. We confess that our own faith looks nothing like His, and we thank You that we are not required to bring His grade of faith. We bring Him. And the faith He exercised at the cross is credited to us by Your grace. Teach us to live this week as people who keep Your commandments and have the faith of Jesus. Where our reach is short, let Your Son's reach cover us. Where our spirit is willing and our flesh is weak, let the angel that strengthened Him in the garden be the encouragement You send us. Where we have been holding out for sight, give us, today, the small and stubborn move of trust that takes us one more step toward home. In Jesus' name, Amen.
