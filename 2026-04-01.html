Abide

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 1, Reality Check Part 5 of 7
April 1, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Jesus used one word ten times in eleven verses because He knew we would spend our whole lives trying to learn what it means.

  • ☩ Abiding in Jesus is not a one-time decision but a continuous, living connection to the true Vine.
  • ☩ The tension: we want fruit in our lives, but we keep trying to produce it on our own instead of staying connected to the Source.
  • ☩ Jesus makes the relationship simple: remain in Him, keep His commands, and His joy becomes yours.
  • ☩ The challenge today is honest self-evaluation: are you truly abiding, or just going through the motions?
🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday we explored God's everlasting, pursuing love, from the intimacy of Eden through Jeremiah's declaration that He draws us with lovingkindness. Today we discover what our response to that love actually looks like in practice. God has always been the One who initiates. Now Jesus gives us the most vivid picture in all of Scripture for what it means to stay connected once He has found us: the vine and the branches. Tuesday established that God's love precedes our response. Wednesday shows us what that response is, reduced to a single word that Jesus repeats ten times so we cannot possibly miss it.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Last Walk Before the Cross

The disciples had just left the upper room. It was Thursday night of Passion Week, and they were walking together through the darkened streets of Jerusalem toward Gethsemane. Within hours, Jesus would be arrested, tried, and crucified. Everything He said on this walk carried the weight of a farewell, and He knew it even if they did not.

As they passed through the Kidron Valley, they likely walked past the massive golden vine that adorned the entrance to Herod's Temple, a famous decorative sculpture with clusters of grapes as tall as a person, symbolizing Israel as God's chosen vine. It was the perfect backdrop for what Jesus was about to say. Israel had long been called God's vine in the prophets, yet the prophets consistently lamented that the vine had gone wild and produced sour fruit (Isaiah 5:1-7; Jeremiah 2:21). Now Jesus made a stunning claim: "I am the true vine." Not the temple, not the nation, not the religious system. Him. Everything His disciples needed for life and fruitfulness would come from staying connected to Him personally.

📖 Into the Word

Today's anchor passage is John 15:1-11, where Jesus delivers the Vine and Branches discourse on the night before His crucifixion. The central question is deceptively simple: what does it actually mean to abide in Christ? The word "remain" in the BSB appears ten times in these eleven verses, making it the unmistakable heartbeat of the passage. We will also look at 1 John 5:3 and Revelation 3:20 as supporting texts that deepen our understanding of what abiding looks like and what happens when we stop.

John 15:1-5
☩ Open in Bible App
1“I am the true vine, and My Father is the keeper of the vineyard. 2He cuts off every branch in Me that bears no fruit, and every branch that does bear fruit, He prunes to make it even more fruitful. 3You are already clean because of the word I have spoken to you. 4Remain in Me, and I will remain in you. Just as no branch can bear fruit by itself unless it remains in the vine, neither can you bear fruit unless you remain in Me. 5I am the vine and you are the branches. The one who remains in Me, and I in him, will bear much fruit. For apart from Me you can do nothing.

The Greek word at the center of this passage is meno (μένω), and it means far more than simply "staying put." It carries the sense of dwelling, remaining permanently, making your home in someone. It is the same word John used when he described two of John the Baptist's disciples asking Jesus, "Where are you staying?" and then going to stay with Him that day (John 1:38-39). Jesus is not asking for occasional visits. He is describing a continuous, moment-by-moment dependence, the way a branch draws sap from the vine without interruption. Notice the organic nature of the metaphor: a branch does not strain to produce grapes through willpower. Fruit is the natural result of connection. Disconnection does not just reduce productivity; it causes the branch to wither and die. The phrase "apart from Me you can do nothing" is not hyperbole. It is agricultural fact applied to the spiritual life. A severed branch does not produce smaller fruit; it produces no fruit at all.

John 15:6-8
☩ Open in Bible App
6If anyone does not remain in Me, he is like a branch that is thrown away and withers. Such branches are gathered up, thrown into the fire, and burned. 7If you remain in Me and My words remain in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you. 8This is to My Father’s glory, that you bear much fruit, proving yourselves to be My disciples.

Jesus presents two outcomes with no middle ground. The branch that remains produces much fruit and brings glory to the Father. The branch that disconnects withers and is gathered for burning. This is not a threat; it is simply what happens to branches that cut themselves off from their life source. There is a beautiful promise tucked inside the warning: "If you remain in Me and My words remain in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you." This is not a blank check for material wishes. When we are truly abiding, our desires begin to align with His, so what we ask for reflects what He already wants to give. The Father's glory is the ultimate purpose of our fruit-bearing, and the word "prove" (or "become") suggests that fruitfulness is not just evidence of discipleship; it is part of the process of becoming a disciple.

John 15:9-11
☩ Open in Bible App
9As the Father has loved Me, so have I loved you. Remain in My love. 10If you keep My commandments, you will remain in My love, just as I have kept My Father’s commandments and remain in His love. 11I have told you these things so that My joy may be in you and your joy may be complete.

Here Jesus reveals the deepest layer of abiding: it is rooted in love, not duty. The love that flows between the Father and the Son, that same quality and intensity of love, is what flows from Jesus to us. "Remain in my love" is not a command to earn love but an invitation to stay inside the love that already surrounds us. And obedience is the evidence that we are remaining, not the price of admission. Jesus kept His Father's commands and remained in His Father's love; we keep Jesus' commands and remain in His love. This is the rhythm of a relationship, not the terms of a contract. The payoff line is verse 11: all of this, every word about vines and branches and commands and love, has one goal: "that my joy may be in you and your joy may be complete." Abiding is not grim religious discipline. It is the path to the deepest joy a human being can experience.

1 John 5:3
☩ Open in Bible App
For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments. And His commandments are not burdensome,

John echoes the same vine-and-branches theology decades later. If abiding in Jesus means keeping His commands, and keeping His commands equals loving God, then the whole thing is relational, not transactional. Notice the crucial last phrase: "His commandments are not burdensome." When religion becomes burdensome, drudgery-like, and heavy, something has gone wrong with the connection. A branch that is genuinely drawing life from the vine does not experience growth as a chore. If following Jesus feels like nothing but obligation, that may be a signal that we have drifted from abiding into performing, and the solution is not to try harder but to reconnect.

Revelation 3:20
☩ Open in Bible App
Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in and dine with him, and he with Me.

This verse comes from Jesus' message to the church in Laodicea, the church that thought it had everything but was actually spiritually lukewarm, wretched, and blind (Revelation 3:17). It is the ultimate picture of what happens when abiding stops: Jesus ends up on the outside of His own church, knocking. The Laodicean condition is the opposite of John 15. Instead of a branch connected to the vine and flowing with life, it is a closed door with Jesus standing on the other side. And yet even here, the grace is staggering. He does not kick the door down. He does not walk away. He stands and knocks, patiently offering to come in and share a meal, the most intimate act of fellowship in the ancient world. The antidote to the Laodicean condition is not more effort; it is simply opening the door and letting Him back in. That is what abiding looks like when it has been lost and then restored.

🎯 Street Level
The Phone That Never Gets Plugged In

You know how your phone works when it is at 100% battery. Everything is fast, the screen is bright, the apps load instantly, and you can handle anything the day throws at you. But somewhere around 15%, things start to change. The screen dims. Apps crash. The phone starts making desperate choices about what to shut down to survive a few more minutes. And if you never plug it back in, eventually it just goes dark.

Most of us would never let our phone die on purpose. We carry chargers, hunt for outlets, borrow cables from strangers in airports. But spiritually, we do this all the time. We run on whatever charge we got from last Sunday's sermon or last month's prayer retreat, and then we wonder why everything feels dim and sluggish by Wednesday. Jesus is not describing a weekly recharge. He is describing a phone that stays plugged in while it is being used. That is what "abide" means: continuous connection, not periodic top-ups. The fruit in your life is not about how hard you are working; it is about whether you are still plugged in.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
America's Disconnection Epidemic Proves Jesus Right

In December 2025, AARP Research published "Disconnected: The Escalating Challenge of Loneliness Among Adults 45-Plus," a survey of over 3,200 Americans conducted by Ipsos. The findings were striking: 4 in 10 adults over 45 now report feeling lonely, up significantly from 35% in previous surveys. Adults in their 40s and 50s are the most vulnerable, and the study found that shrinking social networks are one of the strongest predictors of loneliness. Community engagement, including attendance at religious services, volunteering, and involvement in local groups, is declining across the board.

The researchers noted something that reads like a commentary on John 15: technology offers the appearance of connection, but it rarely creates the deep, sustained relationships that actually address loneliness. People who are already disconnected tend to turn to passive activities like scrolling social media, which provide a feeling of proximity without genuine attachment. Nearly a quarter of lonely adults expressed interest in AI companionship as a substitute for human relationship. Jesus' vine-and-branches metaphor speaks directly into this cultural moment. Surface-level connection is not abiding. Proximity is not relationship. What the research calls "meaningful, in-person interactions," Jesus called remaining: a real, living, reciprocal bond that actually sustains life.

✝️ The Christ Connection

Jesus did not say "I am like a vine." He said, "I am the true vine." He is not offering an illustration; He is making an identity claim. Every branch in the Old Testament vineyard that went wild and sour finds its correction in Him. He is the vine that Israel was always supposed to be, and the only way we bear fruit is by drawing our life directly from Him.

And consider the timing: He spoke these words hours before the Cross. The vine was about to be crushed. The One who said "remain in me" was about to be torn from His disciples, buried in a tomb, and seemingly cut off from everyone. Yet it was precisely through the crushing, through the Cross and the resurrection, that the vine became capable of sustaining every branch for all of history. His death was not a severing; it was a planting. And now, risen and alive, He offers what no dead religious system ever could: a living connection that produces real fruit, real joy, and real transformation. Every other source of spiritual life eventually dries up. He never does.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Set a recurring alarm on your phone for three different times today (morning, midday, and evening). When each alarm goes off, pause for 60 seconds, take a breath, and silently pray one sentence: "Jesus, I am choosing to remain in You right now." Do not add anything else. Do not make a list of requests. Just reconnect. Three times. Sixty seconds each. This is what it looks like to practice abiding in the middle of a real day.
🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the true Vine, and we confess that we have spent too many days trying to produce fruit on our own. We have treated our relationship with You like a weekend appointment instead of a continuous connection. Forgive us for the times we have let the door close and tried to live on yesterday's grace. Teach us what it really means to remain, to dwell in You the way a branch draws life from the vine, moment by moment, without straining or performing. Thank You that Your commands are not a burden, that Your yoke is easy, and that the goal of all of this is joy, Your joy, made complete in us. We open the door today. Come in. Stay. We want to abide. In Jesus' name, Amen.
