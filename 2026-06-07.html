Life's Storms

Growing in a Relationship with God Lesson 11, Setbacks Part 2 of 7
June 7, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Water is coming over the sides, the boat is going under, and Jesus is asleep in the back with His head on a cushion. The disciples wake Him with an accusation, not a request. "don’t You care that we are perishing?" He stills the storm with two words, then asks the question that outlasts the weather. Why were you afraid? The terror was never really about the wind. It was about the suspicion that the One in the boat with them had stopped caring, and a setback is the fastest way to find out what we secretly believe about God.

🔁 Connection to Yesterday

Yesterday we said setbacks are not heaven's report card, and we promised to keep our faces turned toward the One who has been in the boat the whole time. Today we climb into the actual boat. The storm on the Sea of Galilee is where that promise gets its first real test, because it is one thing to say God is with us in the storm while we are sitting on dry land, and another to say it with cold water filling the hull and the only divine help on board sound asleep.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
The Boat Sat Low in the Water

In 1986 a drought dropped the level of the Sea of Galilee and exposed a fishing boat buried in the mud since the first century. When archaeologists pulled it free, it measured about twenty-seven feet long and seven and a half feet wide, with sides that rose only a few feet above the waterline. A boat like that held a working crew with room to spare, but it was never built to fight a real squall. In a heavy sea the waves did not need to capsize it. They only needed to keep breaking over the low sides faster than the men could bail.

That is the boat in this story, and the detail Mark gives about Jesus matters once you can picture it. He was in the stern, the raised platform at the back where the steersman sat, asleep on the helmsman's cushion. The safest, driest spot in a small boat, and the spot a tired traveler would take to rest. Meanwhile the order He had given, to cross to the far shore, was an order to leave familiar Jewish territory and head for the Gentile side of the lake. They were not caught in a storm by accident on the way to nowhere. They were obeying Him when the water started coming in, which is exactly when a storm does its worst damage to faith.

📖 Into the Word

Mark tells the whole thing in seven fast verses, and he does not soften the disciples' panic or tidy up their accusation. Watch where the fear actually points. It is not finally at the wind. It is at the Man sleeping through it, and at what His sleep seems to say about whether He still cares.

Mark 4:35-41
☩ Open in Bible App
35When that evening came, He said to His disciples, “Let us cross to the other side.” 36After they had dismissed the crowd, they took Jesus with them, since He was already in the boat. And there were other boats with Him. 37Soon a violent windstorm came up, and the waves were breaking over the boat, so that it was being swamped. 38But Jesus was in the stern, sleeping on the cushion. So they woke Him and said, “Teacher, don’t You care that we are perishing?” 39Then Jesus got up and rebuked the wind and the sea. “Silence!” He commanded. “Be still!” And the wind died down, and it was perfectly calm. 40“Why are you so afraid?” He asked. “Do you still have no faith?” 41Overwhelmed with fear, they asked one another, “Who is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?”

The words Jesus speaks to the sea are blunt. The Greek behind "Be still" is pephimoso, which means "be muzzled," the word you would use for strapping a muzzle on a dangerous animal. He does not coax the storm down. He collars it like something that has gotten loose. Then He turns to the men and asks why they were afraid, which sounds harsh until you notice what their fear had done. They had been awake bailing while He slept, steering by the one thing they could see, the rising water, instead of trusting the One they could not yet understand. That is the gap Paul names when he says we walk by faith and not by sight (2 Corinthians 5:7). Their real question, "don’t You care that we are perishing?" is the question every setback eventually asks out loud. The calm came in seconds. The deeper lesson took the rest of their lives.

🎯 Street Level
Left on Read

You send the text that actually matters. Not a meme, the real one. "Can we talk?" or "I got the results." Then you watch the little word under it change to Read, and nothing comes back. Five minutes. An hour. And in that silence your mind writes the whole story. They are angry. They are done with you. They never cared as much as you did. By the time they finally reply, "so sorry, I was driving, of course I'm here," you have already lived through an entire falling-out that only ever happened inside your own head.

That gap between the read receipt and the reply is exactly where the disciples were living in the back of the boat. The silence did not create their fear that Jesus had stopped caring. It exposed a fear that was already in them. A setback works the same way on us. The bill, the diagnosis, the prayer that goes quiet, none of it tells us anything new about whether God cares. It just reads our own screen back to us. The hard thing is not proof that He has gone cold. It is a mirror showing what we were already half-believing about Him in the quiet. And His answer, when it lands, is the same one every time. I was never gone, and of course I am here.

🌍 Today's World, Ancient Truth
The Buffer You Bring Aboard

Researchers can now watch this happen in the body. In a standard lab protocol called the Trier Social Stress Test, scientists put volunteers through a deliberately brutal few minutes, a surprise speech in front of stone-faced judges, then mental math out loud while someone corrects every mistake, and measure the stress hormone cortisol spiking in their saliva. The single biggest thing that flattens that spike is not how hard the task is or how much money or status the person has. It is whether they believe they have people in their corner. In a 2018 study by Hooker and colleagues, volunteers who felt well supported showed almost the same calm cortisol response whether they ranked high or low in social status, and strong perceived support all but erased the gap. And the key word is "perceived." It is not finally about who is actually standing there. It is about what you already believe, walking in, about whether anyone has you.

That is the disciples in the stern, almost to the decimal. The danger in the boat was real, but the thing that set their hearts pounding was the suspicion that the Man asleep on the cushion had stopped having them. The storm did not plant that fear. It pulled it up out of soil where it was already growing. This is why a setback feels like proof: the rising water reads back to us whatever we were already half believing about whether God is in our corner. Jesus goes straight to it, past the weather, with His question. Why are you afraid? He is not asking about the wind. He is asking what they had decided about Him before the first wave ever broke.

✝️ The Christ Connection

The disciples end the story with a question they cannot answer. "Who is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?" Anyone raised on the Hebrew Scriptures would have felt the ground move under that question, because in the Old Testament only One ever speaks to the sea and is obeyed. The God who set the boundary the waves cannot cross was now asleep on a cushion in a borrowed fishing boat, tired enough to need the rest, human enough to feel the exhaustion. Hold that picture, because it turns over before the gospel ends. There is a second storm coming, in a garden and then on a cross, and in that one the roles reverse. This time it is the Son crying out, asking whether He has been forsaken, while heaven stays silent and does not still the wind. He let that storm run all the way through Him so that He could answer our "don’t You care that we are perishing?" with His own scars.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
The next time panic spikes today, over a bill, a diagnosis, a text you did not want to get, catch the first sentence your heart fires off. If it is some version of "You must not care," stop and say one sentence back to Jesus before you say another word to the storm. Out loud if you can. "I do not understand this, but I know You are in the boat."
🙏 Closing Prayer

Jesus, we have prayed like the disciples more times than we want to admit, waking You up with an accusation already loaded. We have looked at the water rising and decided Your silence meant You had stopped caring. Forgive us for steering by what we can see. When the next storm breaks over our low sides, remind us who is asleep in the stern, the same One who set the limits the sea cannot pass. Teach us to bring You our fear instead of our blame. In Jesus' name, Amen.

💬 Discussion Question
"When your last real storm hit, what did your gut accuse God of (not caring, being asleep, punishing you, having walked off)? This week, when the fear spikes again, what would it look like to bring Him that exact accusation as an honest prayer instead of a verdict you have already reached?"
