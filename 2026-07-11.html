Unity in Christ

The Essence of Christian Life and Witness Lesson 3, Unity in Christ Part 1 of 7
July 11, 2026
✦ ☩ ✦
📌 TL;DR

Picture a church where people wear a favorite teacher's name like a team jersey, one crowd for Paul, another for Apollos, each sure they picked the winner. That was Corinth, and Paul treats it as an emergency. Before he corrects a single belief, he goes after the divisions, because a church split into fan clubs has forgotten what it is. This week he joins no camp and instead pulls everyone back to the one name that was ever crucified for them. The church was never built to rally around a leader, only around the Lord crucified for it.

🔁 Connection to Last Week

Last week the cross stood alone at the center, the one message Paul refused to dress up or trade away, God's power hidden inside the very thing the world called weak. We ended on a hard line, that humility, not intelligence, is what decides who receives it. Now watch where Paul carries that cross next. Not into a debate with philosophers, but into a church quietly tearing itself apart, members lining up behind their favorite teachers like rival fan clubs. His answer to their divisions is the same cross we just studied. Before he untangles a single quarrel, he reminds them that only one name was ever nailed up for them, and it was not Paul's.

🗺️ Historical & Cultural Context
Everyone in Corinth Wore Someone Else's Name

A man in Roman Corinth rarely introduced himself by what he did. He told you whose he was. This was a city wired for patronage, held together by a web of powerful patrons and the clients who leaned on them for money, protection, legal muscle, and a foothold in the world. Clients gathered at the patron's door before dawn, walked him to the forum, cheered when he spoke, and carried his name through the streets like a badge. Your patron was your identity and your insurance at once. And because standing ran on it, the whole system bred rivalry. To belong to one great man was, quietly, to be measured against the followers of another.

Now drop a young church into that world. These were people who had spent their whole lives sorting themselves by whose name they carried, and old reflexes die hard. They had swapped their patrons for apostles, but they were still keeping score. One group flew Paul's banner, another Apollos's, another Cephas's, each certain their teacher outranked the rest. They had turned the men who first pointed them to Jesus into competing celebrities, and the family of God back into a status game. Paul's whole answer this week is to remind them that only one name was ever nailed to a cross for them, and no apostle's will do. The name you wear is supposed to be His.

📖 This Week's Memory Text
1 Corinthians 1:10
☩ Open in Bible App
I appeal to you, brothers, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you agree together, so that there may be no divisions among you and that you may be united in mind and conviction.

Notice how Paul opens. Not with a command but a plea. The word is parakaleo, Greek for coming alongside someone to urge and steady them, the same root that names the Holy Spirit as the Helper called to our side. Paul could have pulled rank as the founding apostle and ordered them to knock it off. Instead he begs them, and he begs "in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ," because that name is the whole point. The cure for a divided church is never a stronger boss. It is a shared Lord. And look closely at what he actually asks for. Not that everyone become identical, not one flavor of personality or opinion, but that they stop splitting into camps and line up "united in mind and conviction," their minds settled on the same Person.

Hold that plea over everything we read this week. Paul names the fracture out loud, a church chanting "I belong to Paul" or "I belong to Apollos" (1 Corinthians 1:12-17). He files their infighting in the same breath as the ugliest sins he can list, right beside envy and malice (Romans 1:29). He calls it what it is, spiritual infancy, a church still stuck on milk because jealousy and quarreling have stunted its growth (1 Corinthians 3:1-4). Then he lifts their eyes to the only mind that heals division, the mind of Christ, who did not grab at status but emptied Himself and obeyed all the way down to a cross (Philippians 2:5-8). And he redraws what leadership even means, not the polished patron the city admired but a man beaten, imprisoned, and worn thin by his care for the churches, filling up in his own body what was still owed in the afflictions of Christ (2 Corinthians 11:23-28, Colossians 1:24). One body, one Lord, one name worth wearing.

🗓️ This Week's Road Map
  • ☩Sunday: The Problem of Cliques in the Church. Paul names the divisions out loud and shows why splitting into camps around leaders is not a small flaw but a denial of the gospel.
  • ☩Monday: Centered on Jesus. The cure for factions is not picking the right leader, but remembering that only Christ was crucified for us and only He is Lord.
  • ☩Tuesday: Wisdom and Maturity. Paul traces the quarreling back to spiritual immaturity, a church still on milk, and calls them up into the wisdom of God.
  • ☩Wednesday: Christlike Servanthood. Real leaders are not celebrities to rally behind but servants and stewards who empty themselves the way Christ did.
  • ☩Thursday: A Lifestyle That Reflects the Cross. True ministry is marked by suffering rather than status, and the cross is the only power that breaks our hunger for rank.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought & Discussion. The week lands on why the world will believe we are His when it finally sees us love one another.
✝️ The Christ Connection

Here is the question to carry all week. What could possibly hold together people this different, this quick to pick sides? Paul's answer is not a better program or a stronger personality. It is a Person, and specifically a Person on a cross. On the night before He died, Jesus prayed for people He had never met, that they would be one the way He and the Father are one, so that the world would believe. He did not pray that we would all think alike. He prayed that we would be His. The cross does what no leader ever could. It takes people who would never have chosen each other and makes them one body by making them all debtors to the same blood. This week, watch every division in Corinth turn out to be the same failure underneath, a set of eyes that slid off Jesus and onto a man. And watch Paul mend them the only way that ever works, by turning every face back toward the One who was lifted up for them all.

✅ Personal Application Challenge
Before Sunday, name your camp. Most of us keep a quiet lineup of the Christians we consider a step below us, the ones in the wrong tradition, the wrong politics, the wrong worship style, the ones who follow the wrong teacher. Pick one person or group you have written off that way. Then do one concrete thing this week to move toward them instead of away, a message, an honest conversation, a real prayer for them by name. You do not have to agree with everything about them to belong to the same Lord they do.
🙏 Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, before we open this letter, we admit how easily we split into sides. We pick our favorites and quietly look down on everyone who chose differently. We have wanted to be right more than we have wanted to be one. This week, show us how much our divisions cost You. Remind us that only one name was ever nailed to a cross for us, and it was not the name of any teacher we admire. Teach us to hold our convictions without turning them into camps, and to love the people You have joined us to, even the ones we would never have chosen. Pull every eye back to Jesus, until He is bigger to us than every side. In Jesus' name, Amen.

💬 Opening Question for the Week
"Where have you let loyalty to a person, a tradition, or a 'side' quietly become part of your identity as a believer, and what would change this week if the only name you needed to wear was Christ's?"
